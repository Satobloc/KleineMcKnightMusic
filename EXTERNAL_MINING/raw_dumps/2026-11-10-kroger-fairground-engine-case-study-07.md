### B. Hook invariant and high-frequency glint

- Four-note hook: `↓↑↑↓`, declared invariant.
- Upper partials at 11, 13, 17, and 19 are reserved for the hook's edges.
- The prompt's organizing rule is that each ascent drop should land above the previous floor.

### C. Aisle ascent

Melody, harmony, and beat packets enter in staggered order. The first verse is a 6/8 bop with a displaced instrumental layer; the prechorus rises through unequal terraces; the chorus preserves a simple melody against a more hostile beat and shadow layer. The second ascent grafts 3+3+2 phrases and overlapping entries.

### D. Descent engine

The bridge disables ordinary section behavior while preserving the hook's identity. The path is explicitly ordered:

`jerk → glide → clickstraight → drop → strain → tipover → spiral → sway → thud → crawl`

Each event has a distinct mechanical / acoustic carrier. Durations are deliberately unequal. As the descent proceeds, the hook contracts from four notes to three, two, then one while vocal entries slow and counterlines multiply. The prompt names steam, gurgle, squeal, tow-chain, wet gear, and pressure release as sound events.

### E. Automata farewell and reentry

A mechanically cheerful dotted march delivers the farewell. A wet-curtain rise cuts back into the prechorus, then the final chorus combines several packets while keeping them distinct.

### F. Deep takeover

The outro fuses electro-illbient, deep dubstep, and stomp; the sub inherits the hook while pitch certainty decays. Instruments shed away, the groove changes gait, and the final texture contrasts an unpitched low-frequency bubble with precise high filigree.

## 4. What this case is useful for

1. **Hook identity across transformations:** the prompt explicitly protects one short motif while changing meter, texture, register, density, and section behavior.
2. **Trajectory as musical form:** the fairground ride is translated into an ordered sequence of mechanical motion rather than represented only by sound effects.
3. **Asymmetric durations:** the descent is defined by unequal event lengths, not a conventional fixed-bar bridge.
4. **Controlled packet collision:** multiple style systems are assigned different functions and failure modes, then recombined without being described as one homogeneous genre.
5. **Return-path design:** the wet-curtain reentry returns to a prechorus point rather than simply restarting the song.
6. **Version divergence:** the same prompt body is associated with different model-generated tag descriptions and two different full-generation durations. This supports the need to treat every output as a separate observation, but does not establish what the audio actually sounds like.
