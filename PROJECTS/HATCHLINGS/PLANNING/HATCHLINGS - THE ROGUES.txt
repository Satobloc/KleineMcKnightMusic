The Rogues: A Subsurface Civilization
The Rogues, also known as the Metalworkers, are an advanced alien civilization inhabiting a rogue planet–a planet that has been ejected from its solar system–characterized by a thick atmosphere, a subsurface ocean, and a unique history shaped by a cataclysmic moon impact.
Physical Appearance and Sensory Perceptions:
•
The Rogues are described as having a distinctive appearance, resembling "mechanically intertwined tubing filled with luminescent beads" that glow in vibrant, near-primary colors [1, 2]. Their forms are fluid and constantly shifting, with hues flowing through intricate mazes across their bodies.
•
They lack visual perception in the human sense. Instead, they rely on a sophisticated sonar-like system to perceive their environment, detecting the positions and movements of objects around them [3, 4]. They are particularly sensitive to the movements of living things, and their language incorporates intricate verb systems to describe various kinds of motion, intention, and interaction [4].
•
While they cannot consciously see light, their emotional states are subtly influenced by bioluminescent patterns, suggesting a subconscious connection to light [5].
World and Environment:
•
The Rogues' homeworld is a rogue planet, significantly larger than Earth, with an atmosphere ten to twenty times thicker [6, 7]. The planet's surface is covered in frozen carbon dioxide, beneath which lies a thick layer of ice. Underneath the ice, a vast, warm subsurface ocean teems with life [1, 6]. The ocean is warmer than expected for a rogue planet, possibly due to geothermal heat flux or a process like neutrino annihilation [4].
•
"Down" for the Rogues is towards the core of the planet, and they float against the underside of the ice, which acts as their "ground" [8]. Denser objects sink towards the core, while less dense objects float upwards.
History and Culture:
•
The Rogues' civilization has been profoundly shaped by a singular event in their distant past: a moon impact that disrupted life and scattered metallic fragments across the subsurface [9, 10]. These fragments, lodged in the ice, formed a mountain range that the Rogues call Moon Mountain [9, 11].
•
The discovery of metal revolutionized their society. Its density allowed it to sink "upwards" toward the core, enabling the development of tools, transportation, and exploration technologies [9, 11]. Metal became the foundation of their power, and their civilization flourished under the control of a single ruling state centered around Moon Mountain [12].
•
The Rogues have a rigidly class-ordered society, with lineage tracing back to groups that existed before the moon impact [12]. Their social hierarchy is based on a combination of strength and proximity to the central empire, suggesting a system of selective breeding or eugenics aimed at securing access to metal [12-14].
•
Their history is marked by conflict and a relentless pursuit of metal, as evidenced by the Rogue History Timeline, which spans thousands of years and includes events like "Stripping the Little Moon," "The Last Portal Battle," and "The Great Insurrection" [14-16]. Their long history suggests a slow, almost glacial, pace of development and social change.
Key Cultural Concepts:
•
The word "science" in their language translates as "deep travels," hinting at their understanding of knowledge as a journey into the unknown [17].
•
Their metalworking is primarily cold-worked, showcasing a mastery of material manipulation without the use of heat [18]. The introduction of human materials technology, specifically heat-based processes, causes fear and awe among the Rogues.
Motivations and Goals:
•
The Rogues are driven by a deep-seated desire to acquire more metal, which they view as a source of power and progress [14]. This ambition leads them to explore the depths of their ocean, to venture through portals, and ultimately to set their sights on the metal-rich asteroid belt of the solar system.
Significance to the Narrative:
•
The Rogues represent a formidable challenge to humanity, with their advanced technology, their unique understanding of the world, and their ambition to expand their influence beyond their own planet. Their presence introduces a new dimension of conflict, raising questions about the future of human-alien relations and the potential consequences of interstellar contact.
•
Their unique sensory perceptions and communication methods challenge the human understanding of intelligence and communication, prompting Luce and the research team to re-evaluate their assumptions and adapt their approaches.
•
The Rogues' history and culture, shaped by a cataclysmic event and a relentless pursuit of resources, offer a thought-provoking parallel to human civilization, prompting reflection on the nature of progress, the impact of technology, and the cyclical patterns of conflict and ambition.

Rogue Sensory Perception
The Rogues possess a unique sensory apparatus, primarily relying on sonar and a subconscious sensitivity to bioluminescence, which shapes their perception of the world [1]. Their sensory experience contrasts sharply with human vision-centric perception [2, 3].
•
Sonar as a Primary Sense: The Rogues use a sophisticated sonar-like system to detect the positions and movements of objects [1, 4]. This is their primary mode of perceiving their environment, allowing them to navigate and interact with their surroundings, especially in their dark, subsurface ocean environment [5].
◦
Motion Detection: Their sonar is particularly attuned to the movements of living things, enabling them to discern intentions and interactions through complex "verbs of motion" [1, 4, 6]. This means that the way they perceive the world is highly influenced by movement [7].
◦
Material Density Perception: Their sonar capabilities allow them to perceive differences in material density and composition, enabling them to navigate their environment and evaluate material properties [8, 9]. They can "see" through materials in ways that humans cannot, especially when using direct contact sensing [10].
◦
Contact Sensing: The Rogues can press their sonar generation organs against a surface, such as rock, metal or ice, to directly detect sound waves and vibrations reflecting back through contact, which allows them to bypass the opacity they would perceive if they were viewing the object at a distance through water. This effectively changes their perception of the material, making it more translucent and revealing internal features [11-14].
•
Subconscious Bioluminescence Sensitivity: While the Rogues cannot consciously see light, their emotional states are subtly influenced by bioluminescent patterns [1, 4]. This suggests an unconscious connection to light, with different patterns triggering different emotional responses [4, 15]. It is not a visual sense, but an emotional one [16].
•
Other Potential Senses: Besides sonar and subconscious bioluminescence sensitivity, the Rogues may have other senses that would complement their environment and evolutionary history. These include:
◦
Electroreception: The ability to detect electric fields, which could be useful for perceiving muscle movements of prey or other Rogues, especially in close proximity [17].
◦
Chemoreception: A highly developed sense of smell, capable of detecting pheromones or other biochemical signals in the water [18].
◦
Magnetoreception: The capacity to sense magnetic fields, aiding in navigation over long distances, given the strong magnetic fields of their planet [19, 20].
◦
Vibration Detection: A lateral line system, enabling them to detect water movements and vibrations from great distances [21].
◦
Thermoreception: The ability to detect thermal gradients, which could help them locate prey or navigate through thermal layers in the ocean [9, 22].
◦
Acoustic Resonance Sensitivity: The capacity to sense subtle acoustic resonances, allowing them to "feel" the structure of their environment [9].
Perception of the Environment:
•
"Upside-Down" World: The Rogues live under the ice and float against it, so that “down” for them is toward the core of the planet and "up" is towards space, which contrasts with human spatial orientation [5, 23].
•
Material Perception: Their sonar gives them a detailed understanding of material densities and boundaries [3]. They experience metal and rock as highly reflective and opaque when viewed from a distance, but more translucent or even transparent when they are touched, depending on the level of contact with the material [10, 14, 24, 25].
•
Atmosphere: The Rogues perceive the atmosphere on the outer surface as a near-silent environment, where sound waves are highly attenuated and don’t travel far, which is a stark contrast to their dense underwater habitat [26, 27].
•
Bioluminescent Sea: While their open sea is full of bioluminescence, they do not see it. Instead, they associate it with the movements of living things, not with light itself, though the light does trigger a feeling associated with that movement [16, 28].
•
Space: When the Rogues have seen stars, they perceive them as graveyards, because the lack of perceived motion in distant bioluminescent objects is interpreted as recently dead things [16, 28].
In summary, the Rogues' sensory apparatus and perception of their environment are fundamentally different from those of humans. Their primary reliance on sonar, coupled with their subconscious sensitivity to bioluminescence and other senses, allows them to navigate and interact with their world in a complex and unique way. This impacts everything from their culture to their technology [29].
