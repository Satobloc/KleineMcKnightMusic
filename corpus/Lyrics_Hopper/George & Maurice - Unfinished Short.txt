George and Maurice
by Nathan McKnight

George and Maurice shared everything; a womb, a teat, a cradle, a heart, a kidney, a bladder, half a liver—
they shared alienation and hatred, existential confoundment, exactly one short-lived relationship, and the
pain of rejection. And against it all, they shared humor, they shared strength.
George awoke early one morning, with the new rays of an old sun shining across his face in all but a long
shadow cast across his eyes by Maurice's bony old nose, and in a shared nursing home bed, George turned
to Maurice.
“Mo,” he said, “Mo. Let's go, Mo. I have to use the bathroom. I figured you could help me.” His chuckle
at the tiny joke woke up his brother, as he knew it would. Maurice's eyes opened. He looked thoughtful,
breathing quietly through his nose. Then, Maurice spoke, saying “Who are you?”
