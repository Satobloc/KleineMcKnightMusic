

TITLE: Crossingtide
(c) 2008 by Nathan McKnight 

Shepherd Quickdart fluttered out of the sediment, stirring up clouds of turbidity that startled the villagers. He smelled the perpetrator in the current from the cool river runoff. He knew his quarry well. He’d been chasing the polyamator as he skipped from estuary to estuary, trying to blend in before he committed his next cross-contamination. 
Quickdart slid along the bottom, quietly swimming upstream. The effort was invigorating. It reminded him of last Opposition's breeding season, when the currents of his home river were especially strong and cool. He missed its smell, missed making love to its waters. But his mind was pulled to the present when caught another whiff of the criminal, shivering with disgust at the thought of the polyamator spawning in *his* river. 
The sediment in this river was thick and murky, typical of the polyamator’s haunts. It made him cough, but he kept quiet as he slithered between the roots of the sand trees. Come breeding time, this estuary would be a throng of spawning families during Opposition, but this close to Passing, it was a ghost town. 
There was a sound to his left, a swish of water, and a startle of motion. Quickdart’s tail snapped, just grazing flesh, drawing blood, then darted in pursuit. The flow of the chase blurred his eyes even as it cleared his gills. The perp dashed under a log, then popped back out at Quickdart’s face and fled over his shoulder. Quickdart turned and pursued him back downstream towards the village, hearts beating in cascade. 
"Choke on sand, Shepherd," shouted the perp, before he disappeared into the crowded market. 

Quickdart swam upside down above the heads of the shoppers, trying to catch the polyamator, or spot him with his eyes. The behavior drew stares from the villagers. He heard the sound of a scuffle at the edge of the market, and darted towards it.

A bone merchant writhed in front of his hole, bleeding from a deep ventral wound. Quickdart rushed up to him and waved a stranger to go find a poulticer. "Which way?" he whispered to the dying merchant. But he was too far gone.

Quickdart swam into the merchant's hole, jangling bone scrapers and needles as he rushed through narrow corridors. There was nobody here, but the hole exited into a deep fauna-encrusted crevasse. A tiny tended garden swayed at the edge of the crevasse, beyond which cruising along the jagged bottom would be impossible. 

Quickdart cursed. Lost him again. 

But just then, he heard the gurgle of somebody struggling to hold their gills shut. 

"Give it up, polyamator," said Quickdart patrolling the edge of the garden, always keeping his body between the merchant's dark hole and the garish fauna. 

"My name is Leantail," he heard a voice say from behind a stand of polypflowers. 

"Whatever your name is, you're out of options. One way or another, it's surrender or fight, so you might as well come out now and we'll get this over with."

"I can still flee," said the criminal, his voice rasping with desperation.

"You've already fled from estuary to estuary, contaminating every river you've touched. Would you appreciate strangers spawning in your homeriver? There's nowhere left to flee."

"You don't get it," said the polyamator, "I'm from the outskirts of the great delta. My little stream is a cesspool from all the riff raff that show up for Opposition. And now, every year, my spawn mingles with millions. I can't even make myself make love to my own water. What am I supposed to do, roll over and die of gametosis?"

"Listen Leantail, I'm from the city too. It's hard, but you have to accept your home for what it is. I did, and so do most of us. And however hard your life is, that's no excuse for spawning in *multiple* streams."

"Well, lots of others die. My estuary is unlivable, my offspring are doomed if I spawn there. Hopping from stream to stream is the only chance I have. I can still flee," he repeated. 

"There's nowhere to go, pervert. Flee into the crevasse if you want. You'll get caught up in the firebranches or sucked up by a hexapus. I'd rather take you into custody, but if you want to kill yourself, that would solve my problem just as easily." 

"I can still go *up*. The worlds are passing tonight, I can cross to the frontier."

"Are you crazy? That's a long swim, even from here. By the time you get to Highpoint, you'll be exhausted. You couldn't possibly make the crossing alive."

"What do you care?"

"Because you're a polyamator and a killer, and I'll be damned if I let you get away, even to the frontier."

The garden was quiet but for the waving of bristleweeds. 

The perpetrator slithered from around the polypflowers. He was a sullen adolescent, his gills lined with dark streaks from mudhuffing. 

Quickdart approached cautiously, feeling sad for the kid. Nothing could end well for this one. He gave Leantail a sympathetic look, but reached out to loop a twine around his forefin. Just when he was within reach, the kid lunged, knocking Quickdart back. His dorsus stung with bristleweed quills and the kid fled into the bright water above. 

Quickdart's Shepherd training kicked in and he swam into the sky in persuit. The village vanished in the depths, and he could hear the astonished gasps of shoppers below. 

If this kid was serious about crossing, he either had some serious grit or he was suicidal. The prospect terrified Quickdart, and he was a trained Shepherd of the Law. When the frontier planet passed overhead and the twin seas kissed, there were only a few minutes to make the crossing, and it was a struggle against a raging current that, when Passing was over, was flung out into space, a monstrous spray of rapidly-freezing seawater. Quickdart had heard stories of whole families ejected into space and raining down again, frozen into gigantic balls of hail. If the sparse reports from those rare souls who claimed to have crossed *back* were to be trusted, you'd find yourself on your own on another planet, in a barren land populated by society's rejects, with nowhere to return to during Opposition. No family stream. No home estuary. Quickdart shivered. 

It was insane, but every year one or two people tried. Most of them were thwarted by the Crossing Guard, and those who evaded them were usually killed in the process of the crossing itself. It was news whenever somebody actually made it across the sea bridge. And whatever happened to them after that, nobody knew.

Leantail thrashed violently, an inefficient swimmer, but he was young. And with his head start and Quickdart's bristle-stinging skin, he was far out ahead. As the hours rolled on, the kid shrunk to the distance, a dark speck soon to disappear. Quickdart whistled the call of the Crossing Guard and swam on.

He thought of his home estuary being polluted or overrun by throngs of strangers. What would he do? Would he keep trying to return home? Would he be able to make love to the water of his own familiar river once it was no longer sweet and cool? It would kill him inside to have to make love to murky water. But what could you do? You have to spawn. Everybody has to spawn. Opposition was half a year away, and the longing for high tide was strong. Quickdart let himself imagine for a moment what it would be like to spawn in a home estuary not his own. The smell of unfamiliar water, the keening of strangers. It made him feel dirty, queasy. He shook off the feeling, ashamed of thinking such sick thoughts. He would rather die of gametosis than commit such a cross-contamination.

He had been swimming for hour upon hour, and the light from the shallows above was dimming. This was the highest he'd ever swam, and he was feeling light headed. His eyes and gills tingled. The Crossing Guard should be close, but he had trouble whistling. A strange whine permeated the water. 

Quickdart strained to see what was happening above. He could see the annual eclipse starting, a massive shadow creeping across the water. 

"Leantail!" he shouted, "Give it up. This is crazy." 

He wheezed with the effort. The water up here was fizzy, and breathing it so hard was pure stabbing pain. Above, he saw saw a group of shadows swimming into the eclipse. As they converged, he started to catch up, and he could make out what was happening. They were going to apprehend the polyamator. Relief flooded over Quickdart.

In spite of the pain, the fizzy water seemed to be energizing, and he swam faster. He wanted to be a Scene of this one. He'd been persuing the polyamator too long to see him captured by volunteers. 

The frontier planet was a behemoth looming above. As its crescent became clearer, the blue of foreign seawater glinted brilliantly. The guards surrounded the kid before Quickdart could get there, and he felt a mixture of relief and disappointment. He slowed down to approach and let them do their job. The kid hovered motionless. He looked tired and Quickdart could see blood seeping from his gills. The guards were talking to him in reassuring tones.

Damnit, thought Quickdart. This wasn't the right approach. He swam up to the group, and one of the guards turned, surprised. 

"Grab him," shouted Quickdart.

But the kid used the distraction to stab two of the crossing guards between the eyes.

Quickdart surged forward and tackled the boy, narrowly dodging the sharp spine of his tail. They wrangled for a moment, but Leantail slipped out of his grip.

"You can't go much higher," shouted one of the guards as the kid swam for the sky with Quickdart in persuit. 

The kid trailed cloud of blood and adrenaline. 

The whine in the water was getting louder. The surface of the sea was in sight, and it roiled. He had never seen it so close before, but he'd always heard it described as mirrory and placid. This was anything but.

The water seemed to curve upwards, into a hill reaching towards the frontier planet above. Leantail swam up into the bulge as it stretched upward, thinner and thinner, like a tunnel. Quickdart could see great masses of water separating themselves from the sea surface and flying up towards the other planet. Currents swirled, carrying foamy white streamers and small fishes. At the surface, he could see floating tree branches and debris circulating. The kid was cornered again. 

"Don't come near me," he said. "I'll kill you."

Quickdart tried to grab him and Leantail tried to stab back, but both of them flailed as a surge of current swept them into the tunnel of water above. A roar rose and the water went white. 

Quickdart's eyes and gills felt like they were going to be pulled out of his head, and he swam upward, feeling frantically around for the kid. 

An alien smell washed over him, and the whiteness passed. Suddenly, he found himself in the center of a vast bridge of perfectly clear, ice cold water. To one side, a planet. To the other side, a planet. Leantail curled, fetal in the middle of it, surrounded by stars. Quickdart's hearts beat in cascade, and he took the kid under his fin. 

From the position of the sun, Quickdart was able to determine which world was his own, and he began to swim towards it. 

The swimming was hard in this corridor, and his fins felt like they would freeze. He swam towards the whitewater below, tugging the unconscious kid along. 

Though he was battered and exhausted, he couldn't help but feel struck through by the beauty of swimming in the sky. He could see the entire world spread below. The blue of ocean, the long peninsula of the continent stretching over the horizon. It was terrifying.

Suddenly, the ocean bridge constricted, and Quickdart instinctually pushed the kid out ahead, towards home. The effort pushed his own body back, towards the frontier. He had felt weightless, now he felt like he was falling, and he swam forwards behind Leantail's limp body. 

In an instant, the wall of ocean surface appeared in front of him. He could see Leantail on the other side of it, in a wobbling tail of water extending to the home world. And he knew he was not going home. Forgetting what that meant, Quickdart turned and swam for his life. The frontier world below seemed to be getting bigger, the water seemed to be getting colder. He glanced back at his home, but was startled by the snap of sea surface closing off again, separating the tongue of water into another floating globe behind him, then another, and another. He imagined himself getting caught in one of those, to freeze into a giant ice ball and spend the rest of eternity in the ice ring around the sun.

He swam as hard as he could into the unknown below, foreign seawater engulfing him. The foamy expanse of whitewater loomed, and then with a concussion that deafened him and knocked his gills shut against his sides, a wall of sea surface cut him off ahead. He reeled. The waterscape around him had coalesced into a wobbling sphere. He swam in a little circle, seeing there was no way out. After a lifetime in open water, it was deeply confusing. The seconds ticked forwards over what seemed like ages. The frontier planet below was growing rapidly, and home was shrinking rapidly. The surface of the mini-sea he floated in seemed to be calming as it fell, and white bubbles of ice seemed to be multiplying along the leading edge. 

Quickdart felt his muscles weakening, and his Shepherd training kicked in. He swam in wide circles, skimming the edge of the sphere, hoping to keep himself, and the water around him from freezing. He couldn't feel his body at all, but he could tell he was moving. Dizzy, nearly blacking out, he swam, swam, swam. Through foggy vision, he saw the frontier planet expand out, flattening.

Then a crushing pressure wave knocked him unconscious. 

The smell of the home estuary filled his gills. He floated, serene. 

Then he blinked awake, bobbing in the sea. Claps of hail thundered in the distance. A surge of flotsam struck him, and he felt bones crunching in his side. He panicked, swimming down. The hailclaps sounded louder and louder. A ball of ice plunged past his face, sinking deep, and he kept swimming. Another plunged past, trailing bubbles. He swam on. His body was numb but his gills felt hot. Huge hail boulders plunged on all sides, and others rushed up from below, flying back towards the surface. The sounds of cataclysm wracked his body, the cracking of ice like the cracking of enormous bones. He wasn't sure if it was the hail stones smashing against each other, or his own body being beaten pulpy. 

Darkness closed. Warm water felt like burning, but it was good. He swam towards the seabottom, the echoes of Passing quieting behind him. He swam as deep as he could until he found a warm current, then relaxed, hoping the current would carry him somewhere. Some foreign estuary he could make his home.
