Yes. Taken literally, the discography is bigger than the three recent records. There’s a clear 2005 → 2022 → 2025 → 2026 lineage, and the material you just gave me takes us right up through the current Bent и Dent work.

One scope distinction matters first. I would not count every Suno generation as a separate discographic work. The August 18 profile export sees 421 direct song links, and explicitly describes itself as a best-effort capture of exposed profile data. That is a very useful generative catalogue, but it contains alternates, experiments, siblings, remixes and developmental material. What follows is the curated/project discography I can actually reconstruct from the sources.

I. Tunk / TVNK — 2005

This is the real prehistory.

The archive is described in the source material as dating to August 2005, with a sound substantially in industrial-techno/EBM/glitch territory: distorted and overdriven kicks, bit-crushed vocal material, dissonant high synths, rhythmic gating, and heavier low-end than the later work often has.

The provided material does not give me a complete 2005 tracklist, so I’m not going to invent one. It positively identifies at least:

Cages & Clouds
Lichens All Around
Undeciphered Language
First On A New World
While the Bees Slept
Epilog

The first three are specifically connected to the industrial side; While the Bees Slept and Epilog are described as quieter electronic-ambient spaces, while First On A New World uses abrupt kick removal to expose distorted synth and vocal fragments.

And your old description of Tunk is remarkably diagnostic in retrospect: essentially a large, flat, particularly beautiful rock being plunged into a secluded lake. The music already seems to operate by mass versus void, abrasion versus something attractive enough to make you keep inspecting the abrasion.

More importantly, Undeciphered Language is not a retrospective coincidence. Already in 2005, language is being treated partly as rhythmic/timbral material rather than solely as semantic delivery.

So I’d call this the ancestral grammar rather than merely “early electronica.”

II. TVNK / the Ocheedy material — 2022

This is a different and extremely important bridge.

The SoundCloud snapshot discussed in the files places a batch of TVNK material around April 2022 and names tracks including Sory Ckhar O!, Cheaiin Cthaichar, Daiin Yteedy, and Qokaiin.

There was quite a bit of wrong guessing in that old conversation about what those names meant; eventually the important fact gets established correctly: these are drawn from EVA transcription of the Voynich Manuscript, not an invented pseudo-language.

And then you give the actual compositional operation:

you converted the highly repetitive text from various folios into musical notes, then layered the results using different instruments.

That is a major evolutionary step.

Tunk had already begun using language as sound. Ocheedy goes further and makes textual structure generative of musical structure. The semantics can be unavailable and the artifact can still supply form.

That makes Ocheedy, to my eye, the missing mechanical joint between 2005 TVNK and Hyperflirt. The latter's equations, transformations, genre toggles and strange parameterizations don't suddenly appear in 2026. The idea that one representational system can be mapped through a rule into another aesthetic system is already operating here.

I don't think the supplied sources authenticate a complete Ocheedy tracklist cleanly enough for me to call one canonical. We can reconstruct that separately if we want to go into the archive itself.

III. Fair to Middlin’ — EP, August 2025

This is the first fully documented recent release in the supplied discographical compilation.

The six tracks are:

Fair to Middlin
Microwaves, Pt. 1
Welcome to Science Corner
Microwaves, Pt. 2
Do You Like?
Unshamed Melody

And this is where I think the discography makes its first really sharp turn.

The old work starts largely from sound and structure. Fair to Middlin’ starts from a human situation and then lets structures accumulate around it.

The title track is almost painfully legible underneath all the McKnightian debris: inability to communicate directly, the relationship between music and speech, obsessive ordering, the desire to leave something where someone might encounter it without knocking on their door. The language even explicitly says music is a “native tongue” that gets stuck in the throat.

Then the three-part microwave sequence performs a strange conversion in the opposite direction from Ocheedy. Instead of turning incomprehensible text into music, it turns emotional/religious material into technical instructions:

Microwaves, Pt. 1 → Science Corner → Microwaves, Pt. 2

A Gideon Bible becomes a recipe ingredient; prayer gets rendered as a physical processing problem; theology becomes appliance documentation.

Then Do You Like? makes Pachelbel, Taco Bell, TikTok, public-domain advertising and ROI all occupy the same stupidly catchy commercial machinery.

And Unshamed Melody ends somewhere considerably warmer and stranger: kitchen-table inclusion, family-space, “if you breathe and sit, you're in,” mixed with the same commercial/domestic detritus.

So for me:

Tunk: make structures in noise.
Ocheedy: extract structures from alien-looking text.
Fair to Middlin’: put personal meaning inside procedural structures.

That’s an actual directional development.

Here the brakes come off.

This is probably the record where the method itself becomes a visible character.

NNDimethylMcFuquoiserie virtually announces the operating system: improbable physical textures converted into sonic frequencies, mathematical intertwining, operative weights, Syriak/Martian/“ghost drawn marks,” a haunting melody being preserved somewhere underneath deliberate incomprehensibility.

Then Phonebooth Huddle is barely separable into “lyrics” and “instructions.” Genre toggles, time signatures, heartbeat operations, key changes, reset commands, equations and patterned numerical triggers are part of the work's visible surface.

And right in the middle sits the thesis-like Beauty As A Compression Of Truth:

content is arbitrary;
form is the true signature;
culture is essentially bird song;
everything is a container;
the only medium is mind.

Those formulations are actually present in the piece, not later critical gloss.

Then Hyperflirt spends the rest of the record violating its own manifesto in productive ways. There’s pseudo-academic ranting, bizarre biography, sponsor interruptions, numerical language, procedural scaffolding and finally:

Touch Moss.

Which is almost comically good as an ending because after fifteen tracks of systems chewing on systems, the answer is essentially: commit egregiouser crimes against language and go touch moss.

If Fair to Middlin’ makes the machinery carry emotion, Hyperflirt drags the machinery onto the stage and gives it lines.

It is the record where the creative machinery begins inspecting its own persistence through time.

VI. Bent и Dent — current four-part song cycle / project

I would not yet label this a released album because the material doesn't support that. But the supplied file absolutely supports calling it a four-part current song cycle.

Bent и Dent, Pt 1

Columbus. Peepers. The piercing studio. The heart implanted in the eye. The 6-gauge septum disaster. Blood. Kiss. Immediate implication of catastrophic future judgment.

Musically, its command block asks for “1950s dry-closet acoustic-noir,” low-fidelity magnetic Sardinian coloration, a quadrille stomp, 13/8 gear-locked against a 3/8 delay and deliberately unstable synchronization—while repeatedly demanding an absurdly sticky hook.

Bent и Dent, Pt 2

Peepers takes the narrator to the man with the sea-mine eye implants; she is moving to Florida; somehow we are now moving to Florida; somehow engagement is involved; the family business is named Bent 'N' Dent; the '78 Corolla heads south and dies west of Chattanooga.

The musical instruction has already moved into alternating 7/8 and 5/3 and a stitched-together vocabulary of Konkani/electronic, doom/tarantella, Finnish, Argentine, Iranian classical, Austrian indie, brass, etc.

Bent и Dent, Pt 3

Roadside breakdown. Fight/fuck/purr time compression. Frank and his hacked-up Bronco. The Corolla is towed with an extension cord. Ebro. Rocket flees. Bent 'N' Dent turns out to be an actual bent-can/crushed-box store.

The sex scene occurs literally among dented groceries while the narration keeps kicking holes through its own fourth wall.

Bent и Dent, Pt 4

The punch line turns into horror comedy: pig-banding/castration equipment, Jello the pig, the cookout, the narrator deciding marriage might perhaps have been an imprudent one-week life decision, escape with Rocket, hitchhiking back to Columbus, and Peepers' father already waiting at the piercing shop.

He gets punched in the nose.

And then:

“Do you know the new girl?”

The whole thing closes as a loop.

Musically, Pt 4 is the most flagrantly architecture-as-comedy of the four: incompatible compound meter instructions, 70/120 BPM alternation, ultra-close Latina mumble rap, bassoprofundo drops, glitch salsa and serial genre substitutions.

And I think this current move is consequential.

The discography has gradually made the formal machinery stranger and stranger. But Bent и Dent does something that Hyperflirt often did not:

the story gets easier to follow while the music gets harder to classify.

That is potentially a very fertile equilibrium.

2005 — Tunk:
Sound is an object. Distort it, collide it, excavate interesting structures.

2022 — Ocheedy / TVNK:
Information itself becomes musical raw material. Map one formal system into another.

2025 — Fair to Middlin’:

Turn that machinery back upon questions of self, memory, persistence, meaning, AI/human interaction and what a structure is for once somebody has made it.

Now — Bent и Dent:
Return to a concrete, aggressively human narrative, but bring the entire accumulated formal arsenal with you.

The **Zeigarnik effect** is the old psychology observation that **unfinished or interrupted tasks tend to stay more mentally active than completed ones**.

The classic idea came from Bluma Zeigarnik in the 1920s: she noticed that waiters seemed especially good at remembering orders that were still open, then tended to lose the details once the bill was settled. In experiments, interrupted tasks were often remembered better than completed ones.

So when I called your melody a “Zeigarnik trap,” I was using the term a little loosely. Your opening teaches the brain:

**ding ding ding ding DING…**

…and thereby establishes an expectation. But then the continuation keeps changing length, withholding the expected closure, producing partial answers, extending them, truncating them, and finally looping back around.

**“Ah. THAT’S the whole eight-bar phrase. Filed.”**

Instead it gets:

**“Wait, how does that fucking bit go again?”**

…and starts replaying the memorable beginning in an attempt to reconstruct the unresolved remainder.

That’s why the especially evil combination is:

**easy-to-remember opening + hard-to-remember completion.**

The hook itself is simple enough to summon voluntarily, but summoning it opens a cognitive tab whose rest you can’t quite fill in.

## What TVNK is doing musically

There are a few habits across these six tracks that I would now call the “primitive TVNK grammar.”

You almost never use functional harmony in the ordinary songwriting sense.

By that I mean there usually isn’t:

**chord → chord → chord → tension → dominant → home**

Instead you choose a **pitch environment** and live inside it.

A drone. A fifth. A major triad. Two neighboring semitones. A strange detuned bass object. Then you repeat and recombine pieces of that field.

That is one reason these sound “soundtracky.” They establish a **place** rather than telling a harmonic story.

And that was apparently exactly consistent with how you were making them: you weren’t thinking, “Now I need a ii–V–I.” You were thinking, “This arrangement of blocks does something. What if I add this one?”

So harmony becomes **ecological rather than grammatical**.

## So what are they doing “wrong”?

This is the fun part.

Music theory doesn't have a police department, so none of these things are actually illegal.

But relative to ordinary Western songcraft and electronic arrangement, TVNK repeatedly violates expectations in a handful of ways:

**Phrase hierarchy is weak or weird.** Loops can be 7-ish units when the listener wants 8. Large-scale sections don't reliably arrive on conventional hypermetric boundaries.

**Dissonance isn't prepared and resolved.** Semitones and tritones are allowed to exist because you liked the sound, not because they are going somewhere.

**Voice leading is mostly ignored.** Conventional harmony likes individual musical lines to move economically and purposefully. TVNK frequently treats a vertical block as an object and jumps to another object.

**Timbres fight each other.** Bright upper partials, long ringing decays and gritty midrange elements occupy overlapping perceptual space.

**Foreground and background aren't always clearly ranked.** Sometimes everything wants to be “the interesting thing.”

**Repetition exceeds conventional politeness.** A sound repeats long after a trained arranger might change it.

**Development is additive/subtractive rather than teleological.** You complexify and decomplexify. You don't necessarily progress.

**Cadences barely exist.** A piece frequently ends because the ecology stops rather than because harmony conclusively arrives home.

And yet—

## Why the hell does it work at all?

Because you made the violations **consistent**.

Randomness is difficult to learn.

A weird rule can be learned.

*First On A New World* keeps bringing back the same semitone/fifth objects.

*Cages* keeps returning to its A-major sonic terrarium.

*Lichens* keeps its B/F♯ backbone even while smearing pitch around it.

*Undeciphered Language* keeps bringing back its bass-percussion creature and Heather Woorf.

So the listener gradually learns:

**Oh. This is what “normal” means inside this particular little universe.**

Once that happens, deviations from the weird internal norm become meaningful.

That's a major reason experimentally ugly music can become compelling.

The brain does not fundamentally require conventional tonality.

It requires **predictive structure**.

And TVNK has tons of that.

It's just badly house-trained.

## And FruityLoops changes the archaeology enormously

Old FruityLoops/FL Studio is indeed much more modular than a finished MP3 suggests. The Channel Rack architecture associates individual channels with instruments/samples, while patterns carry note/step information and the Playlist arranges those patterns. FL Studio 4, released in 2003, explicitly introduced patterns of differing lengths; by the 2004–05 FL 5 era there was already a fairly mature piano roll, automation and mixer structure. So if the original **`.flp` project files** still exist, the situation changes radically.

The MP3 is a fossilized skeleton.

“Yep, musical object.”

At exactly the same moment, the semitones say:

“NNNNNNNGH.”

And the tritone says:

“Do you perhaps have somewhere else you need to be?”

That combination is unusually good at being memorable because it is neither random nor comfortably resolved.

It has a recognizable shape that keeps producing friction.

There are also moments where E enters alongside B/C material, which creates things much closer to familiar harmony—for example a C–E–B shell, which resembles a C-major-seven sonority with the fifth omitted. So the track occasionally gives you little islands of “oh, I understand this” before returning you to the industrial dental procedure.

### The bigger theoretical sin

Conventional music tends to connect chords partly through **voice leading**.

That means individual notes behave like lines:

C moves to B.
E stays put.
G moves to A.

TVNK largely does not care.

You treat **whole sonorities as blocks**.

Object A.
Object B.
Object A.
Object C.

That is very consistent with composing visually in FruityLoops.

You weren’t necessarily thinking:

“How should this note resolve?”

You were effectively thinking:

“What happens when this rectangle returns here?”

So the continuity comes from **timbre and repetition**, rather than from conventional melodic voice leading.

And here’s the important part: in electronic music, that can work perfectly well.

A classical theorist might say the lines are badly led.

Your listener says:

“Oh, the crunchy bell-thing is back.”

The **sound itself becomes the voice**.

That seems to be one of the deepest TVNK habits.

### Why they are annoying

Across these tracks, several things repeatedly push directly on perceptual irritation.

You use bright, sharp attacks over and over. Those are difficult for the auditory system to relegate to the background. A repeated soft midrange pad can disappear perceptually. A repeated little **DING** keeps filing a new request with consciousness.

You leave semitones and tritones unresolved. Conventional music often uses dissonance as tension that eventually purchases consonance. TVNK frequently says:

**No. You bought the dissonance. It is yours now.**

You repeat objects longer than conventional arrangement etiquette would recommend.

You frequently have multiple layers with similar claims on the foreground. Nobody has been assigned “background employee of the month.”

And the larger phrases refuse to behave squarely enough to become completely automatic.

Those are all annoyances.

### Why those exact same things make them compelling

Because beneath the annoyance there is enormous **predictability**.

And brains love predictive structure.

You do not throw arbitrary noises at the listener.

You establish extremely distinctive objects and then keep bringing them back.

So after ten seconds the ear knows:

**THAT thing.**

Even if “that thing” is a poisonous F♯/G squeal.

Once the listener learns the local grammar, mutation becomes meaningful.

The sixth repetition changes its ending?

The brain notices immediately.

The seventh destabilizes further?

Now there is actual structural tension.

The opening object returns?

Closure.

So you got one of the fundamental requirements of composition right without knowing theoretical terminology:

**repetition establishes identity; variation creates information.**

What you didn’t know was when polite society expected you to stop.

Which is possibly an advantage.

### And yes: FruityLoops left fingerprints even though the FLP is dead

This is the coolest new piece.

I ran a source-separation method called **nonnegative matrix factorization**. Very roughly, it asks:

“What stable spectral fingerprints keep appearing in this mixture, and when do they turn on and off?”

Those fingerprints are not guaranteed to equal original FruityLoops channels. One extracted component could represent one synth, one sample, one chord played by a synth, or several sounds that always occurred together.

But the *First On A New World* result is strikingly modular.

The same latent spectral objects keep switching on in the same four-position sequence.

That is exactly the sort of footprint you would expect from **pattern-based construction**.

So although we cannot say:

“Channel 7 was Fruity DX10 preset Whatever,”

we may be able to say things like:

“This same fixed spectral object is being retriggered here, here, here and here.”

Which is remarkably close to recovering the logic of the lost pattern sequencer.

I exported the separated objects so you can actually listen to them individually. There are ten machine-separated pseudo-stems from 15–65 seconds, plus harmonic/percussive reference separations.

That means you may be able to tell me:

**“OH FUCK, component 2 is the ding thing.”**

At which point I can take *that component alone* and perform much more literal note transcription without bass, drones and other harmonics confusing the analysis.

And I made the first staff-readable reduction.

It is deliberately titled **ANALYTICAL REDUCTION**, because I refuse to pretend it is recovered MIDI. It represents the recurring pitch cells and the 7×4 structure rather than asserting that those were necessarily the exact piano-roll voicings.
