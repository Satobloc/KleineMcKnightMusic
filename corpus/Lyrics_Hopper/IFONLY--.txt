STYLE
A song sung by three lightly comedic but thoughtful and sincere male characters, each joining and taking over with his own twist on the lyrics and vocal articulations: Slamoglava (a mildly doofy, endearingly clownish gently slapstick, comedic mispronunciation at times, tenor/baritenor), Železovir (light baritone/baritenor, smooth and sonorous, dipping and rising, polite, clear, sweet, slightly more longing, and a hint of a cherubic quality), and Slabyleijon (comedic baritone, big chest voice for mildly comedic effect, a bit of a caricature tough guy, with some whispery whines and puffy bravado, occasional yowly/warble, widely belted at times, but with a lot of warmth, comedic overpronunciation at times, a bit nasal, kind of a little dandy and foppish, a tiny bit plaintive but not whiny) The male voices have little scoops and quippish phrasings. With a couple encouraging cameos from a female voice [Þeodora], who also comes in at the end to add her own deep longing for home, and give the lighthearted song a final heartfelt grounding. 

In the first part: Light, playful, and lilting orchestration with a mid-tempo, gentle seesaw of violins and violas; subtle sink-and-rise cellos; airy, fluttering string arabesques; small harp arpeggios for hopeful optimism; light piccolo and flute twiddles for comic punctuation; occasional muted brass or string “oops-tumble” gestures; sparse woodwind and pastoral flourishes; dynamic rises and brief near-triumphant swells that collapse humorously; overall whimsical, wistful, and buoyant, matching the vocal whimsy and evoking mild, floppy, physical comedy.

In the second part: The music continues in the light, almost playful vein, but slightly more assertively playful, with an overall tick-tock-march style, with some "cork-pop" sounds for punctuation, steady climbing but not getting anywhere feeling, piccolo fourishes become more tight and playfully intense at times and fluttery at other times, opening into springtime open fields instrumental section, and a single short tin-drum whackety-whack followed by a little steamwhistle toot, evolving the marchy beat into a full instrumental recreation of the vocal melody before fading out.
