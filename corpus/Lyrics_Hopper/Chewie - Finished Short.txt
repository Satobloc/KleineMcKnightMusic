
TITLE: Chewie
(c) 2005 by Nathan McKnight
A story in 7 scenes.


Intro to "Chewie" by ChatGPT

Step into the grotto of Stig, a gnome with a peculiar devotion and an even stranger purpose. With vivid prose and humor dark as the underground, McKnight weaves a tale of faith, folly, and the mysteries of a world all his own.

Scene 1. THE GROTTO

	Stig sat up in bed, shaking and ashamed. The scantily clad beard-nixies fled as his dream haze cleared. The room convulsed. The diorama he'd made from buttons and plastic dinosaurs fell, taking his felten cap with it. He yipped a feeble "Oh my!" then snatched up the cap, clutching it to his chest as the quake subsided.
	Leaning back with a sigh, Stig picked up his old jimson pipe from the nightstand.
	He would have to clean up after morning smoke, he thought, stroking his century-long beard and lighting up. He suckled the acrid smoke and sparkles infused his vision. Images of nixies danced in the harlequin tapestry behind his eyelids.
	Then a lurch and shudder knocked the smoke out of him and the nixie visions fled again. 
	"This is quite definitely out of sorts!" he protested, hopping out of bed with a scowl. 
	He rubbed the sleep out of his eyes, troubled by a disturbing click-clickity-click echoing down from the grotto.
	He padded through the soft corridor, trying to sidestep piles of moosh, but the shaking knocked him to the floor and he had to use his hands the whole way. By the time he got there, he was exhausting his repertoire of ancient gnomish curse words. When he finally made it to the end of the corridor, he wiped his hands on his overalls and yanked on the tail of the hairball dangling from the ceiling.
	He pulled himself up, whispering the familiar oath: "Mistress Cindy, please forgive me my unclean dreams. For your slightest whim, I would gladly give my--" but he was cut off by yet a third tremor.
	The hairy rope swung and Stig held tight. 
	He climbed on, squishing his way through the tight passage in the ceiling. Fluid treacled into his eyes, and he held his breath, reaching through the squishy passage, wriggling himself upwards until he emerged, surfacing in the Reflecting Pool.
	Sallow bile lapped at the base of the imposing Bezoar, his hand-woven monument to beloved Miss Cindy. Stig's nose twitched at its embarrassing unfinishedness, as it had been doing now for too long. 
	The clickety-click echoed around the chamber.
	But there was something else--Stig furrowed his eyebrows--an odd glint on the waves.
	The Reflecting Pool had ever changed from blue to pink to green with the sweet rains that poured down. But this, this un-color glow was altogether different.
	Her Greatness's grotto's lovely darkness needling with preternatural light, filtering down through the crenellated dome ceiling... He held up his hands and though his eyes were closed, he could clearly see the bones of his fingers.
	Could it be? 
	It was!
	At long last, sign from Cindy!
	"Hallelujah," he breathed, voice jumping to a shout, "Hallelujah! Praised be!"
	He clutched his overalls and to his surprise, the medallion he kept tucked away in his pocket began to warm against his chest. His fingers prickled through the fabric as he grasped its dull edges. He'd prayed to the medallion nightly since he found it, but since the hairfalls had lately failed, he'd begun to doubt its power. 
	"Dearest Miss Cindy," he said, kneeling.
	A last click, cut him off, and the light snapped out.
     Stig splashed to the quivering base of his beloved's Bezoar.
     "O Miss Cindy! I have seen your magnificent light. Your revelation shall not go unheeded!"
     "This is a great day in the Reflecting Pool," he whispered over the quiet drip-dripping.
	Stig put his hand to his mouth and looked around, shooting glances all about the waves. Surely, the ethereal light had occasioned a hairfall as the cool sweet rains always had.
     He ran his hands through the slippery liquid of the pool. Sure enough, there were some hairs. But just a few pitiful bits.
     Stig pouted.
     Where was the angel hair? It had been weeks since a bona fide hairfall. Had he not witnessed a miracle? How was he to complete the long-delayed bezoar?
     With one hand, he clutched the hairs, and with the other, he tugged his beard. He had worked so long to repay his boon from Miss Cindy. The great lady had always supplied the material for his work. He'd thought it certain that this Bezoar would in the end be the finest shrine ever built by gnomish hands.
     Now here it lay, unfinished.
     Now the golden fleece rained no more.
     Now Stig sagged, rubbing his brow.
     Might this have been something other than a miracle? The troubling thought crept across his mind. Could the tremor and glow not be taken instead as...as a warning?
     Goosebumps stippled his neck.
     "No," he said firmly. "I shall not have dark thoughts."
     He looked at the wisps of angel hair, frowned.
     Maybe--he reshaped his mind around a more hopeful idea, a shrewd expression crawling across his face--I wonder if maybe these few golden strands won't just suffice.
     He cursed his faithlessness.
     Yes-yesss, that was it! The Lady had provided him with the workings of a true miracle; one to magnify Her glory.
     Stig bunched the wisps of hair in his palm, then scoured every oozing surface of the Reflecting until he was certain he had gathered every last hair, long or short, regardless of frizz or fray.
     Holding the golden fleece high, and with great reverence, he made a most sincere effort at weaving the them into the Bezoar.
     His fingers were deft, fluttering over the split ends, hoping gently to mend and extend them with his stroke.
     Trying at it for a long stretch with bated breath, all he succeeded in weaving was a sickly straggle that marred the delicate curlicued surface of the great unfinished bezoar.
     Stig frowned.
	"That won't do," he muttered into his beard, "No-no n'no! That simply won't do at all."
	He put his hand to his face.
     "Dearest Cindy, what ever shall I do?"
	Lo these long years had he lovingly woven the bezoar--from its first spindly fibers, braiding in humble thanks to the merciful Miss Cindy, whose sacrifice had saved and elevated even as he was smoked out of his previous home.
	He closed his eyes and canted gnomish cants.
     "One last rain of your angelic fleece would be quite enough, I think," he said to himself. "Enough to adorn your Bezoar with a proper mantle--though it be pitiful, it is holden, I think, of such promise. Such promise."
	He turned his face upward.
	"A I so unworthy? My Liegess...give me a sign!"
     He folded chants into ancient Gnomish tongues, once, twice, thrice--then oncemore, twicemore, thricemore.
     And as he began the third three, a rumble rolled from above. The crenelated ceiling bulged inwards.
     The ceiling was ever in flux--he gasped--but the scale...

Scene 3. TORTURE CHAMBER

	The dome undulated above, glistening. Stig patted the medallion, feverishly whispering words whose meanings had long been forgotten. 
	Of an instant, a gargantuan silven blade sliced a gash through the dome, narrowly missing Stig's nose--but lopping off a lock of his head hair.
     Stig screamed and reflexively snatched up the lock of silver hair, then scuttled into the little alcove behind the Bezoar while the blade sliced forth.
	Bracing air flooded in from the gape above. Stig held his palms against the precious Bezoar, shadowed from the harsh outside light. The great blade finished its cut and withdrew. 
     Stig hunched over the medallion, rubbing it worrystone-style, the hairs in his hands bristling against its silkenhard surface. He turned his back on the bezoar. Whatever was happening, he didn't want to see.
     But even with ducked head, the image of the bezoar in terrifyingly bright light played, awfully, across his inner eye.
     He'd been keenly aware that the Bezoar was unfinished. And this queer milky light showed it for true. A big fat felted hairball. He pressed his head on his hands in despair, sobbing lips sparking against the medallion. 
     "Oh, the folly," he muttered, "why did I build this bezoar? To so call down such frights upon myself!"
     The floor shook, throttling open his eyes.
     Through the new hole in the great dome reached a giant set of fingers, their heinous knuckle hairs visible through parchmentlike gloves.
     Stig shuddered.
     The fingers plunged--obscene--into the grotto, penetrating his holy heart of worship. They clamped down--tightly--terribly--onto the Bezoar.
     And they tugged.
     Stig's jaw hung slack.
     The Bezoar began to rise, its golden lumpiness raining aloft.
     No, he thought. "No!" he shouted, fumbling the medallion back into his pocket.
     He stood. Astonished, indignant.	
	Forgetting his fear, Stig thrust himself forward, digging weaver-thick fingers into the wet fuzz of the Bezoar. He pulled, twisting his hands in the fibers until they seemed permanently bent fistwise.  
     But the giant was too strong.
     Torn from Stig's grip, it left fistsfuls of clotted angel hair.
     When the giant had fully dislodged Stig's Bezoar from its esteemed placement across the Pyloric threshold and the Pool of Light rippled emptily, Stig grasped his hat in disbelief, not bothering to wipe away the droplets splashed upon his face by the bile sluicing from the Bezoar.
     He shook both fists.
     "It's....not.....FINIIIIIIISHED!" he screamed, righteous anger boiling from his spleen. 
	He stomped and cursed, bit his lip...and then...
	...he leapt.
	Cold air stung his wet face. 
	All of his senses: fish-eyed, but crystal clear. 
	And he got it!  
    	The giant wrenched the Bezoar--and Stig along with it--from the sustaining bile he'd called home for years.
	Its shiny hand slung them both carelessly into a chill chromen basin. Stig's head thwacked against metal, and he passed briefly into night.
	Some moments later, teeth aclatter and echoing against thin metal walls, Stig awoke. 
	He stood and spun, seeing nothing but his own distorted reflection. The mirror walls were half again his own height, but he found he could jump and just barely see out of the basin, landing each time with a tinny boot-tink.    
	The first jump saw the blade-wielding giant, dressed in periwinkle robe, a mask over its muzzle. On the second, he saw at least two others abreast a long table. And on the third, a hint of curly blonde hair.
	Golden Fleece on the head of an angel.
	He gasped and ducked behind the still-steaming Bezoar.
	The sight of the golden locks--in situ--that is what gave him the strength to do what he did next.
	Wincing at the sacrilege, Stig climbed atop the Bezoar and gazed down upon the breathtaking face of his sleeping Miss Cindy, his liegess, lying helpless under the knife of this horrid giant and its gaggle of hench-colossi. An enormous tray of torture implements left no doubt as to their intentions.
	Stig winced.
	The effort of the jump and climb had warmed him some, but his hands were still chilled to the bone and he reached for the medallion, hoping it might be hot again.
	In so doing, Stig's trembling knocked the lock hair out of his chest pocket. He cursed and bent to pick it up, his mind incised with the image of the blade that had severed it.
	The infernal giant destroyed everything!
	He scuttled back into the shadow of the Bezoar and squatted, chewing his woody-waxen nails tearing them to the quick. He was unfeeling of the pain, aware only of the abomination that was being performed upon his Heavenly Mistress incarnate. He tugged his beard hard, pulling his mouth into word shapes.
	The agoric cold seeped into his core as he wept into his fists, soaking the lock in brine.
	And suddenly, he cursed his stupidity.
	Of course! How had he not thought of it?
	The Bezoar--it would never be finished without a sacrifice.
	All these long years, his hands might well have been idle. He scowled at himself in chrome-distorted reflection.
	Yes. That was it!
	He stood straight and grasped the medallion for strength, but recoiled. No, he thought, no--although it had once seemed a harbinger of hairfalls, it had become a damnable distraction. Instead of working his way through the problem, he had poured his despair into the thick metal disk, fidgeting away his energy while the bezoar lay, unfinished.
	He cursed the day he found the medallion, tugged it out of his chest pocket, and tossed it clanking onto the floor of the basin. It gave a plaintive spark as he kicked it away.
	He had no more use for trinkets. 
	Steeling himself, Stig wrapped the bezoar's curly tail in his hand. The wiry silver of his lock of hair sparkled against the bezoar's golden fleece. 
	Chewing his tongue, Stig climbed up to the rim of the basin, straddled it, then lowered himself over the edge, inching down the felted hair, until he was standing on a sturdy blue linen pad.
	He pulled hard on the bezoar's tail, putting his whole weight into it. As the golden blobbiness rose above the basin's lip, it wobbled, tottered, and finally fell, landing with a plop.
	His chest heaving, he surveyed his options. Below, a pocket of Cindy's gown hung providentially open. He reflexively reached for the medallion to think--but shook off the spell. How to get the bezoar down there? 
	No time to ponder.
	Time to do. 
	He toed the edge of the fabric and imagined hurtling himself onto her thigh. No. Wait. The gargantuan tray of torture implements lay spread like a market below. That would be an easier jump, but it would be a long fall if he missed. 
	He lowered the bezoar over the great blue linen cliff. He followed with his feet, shimmied until is legs hung far out over the blades, and kept going until he was bent at the waist.
	Biting his lip, Stig thought back how far he'd come with Cindy's help, then pushed off and tumbled through open air.

	She feels her lips, her cheeks, her nostril hairs, up the bridge of her nose--she's aware of her fingerwanderings, more aware than ever, more aware too of the errant hairs in her eyebrows. Some of them are growing in stubby from the last time she plucked them with dent-worn fingernails.
	Her eyelids too, are punctuated with stubby lashes, poking at the tips of her fingers. They feel wrong, and she can't resist yanking one out.
	Instead of putting it to her lips, she rolls that one hair between thumb and forefinger, then thumb and middle, thumb and ring, thumb and pinky, until she falls asleep.

	"Wake up hon," says the nurse.

	Under the sheets, Cindy runs her fingers covertly over her pubes, selecting one to pluck and roll. She doesn't do anything else with it, just flicks it away after bit. Whether she's under disapproving gazes or not, the unconforming hairs have to go. Like slivers stuck in skin, they must be plucked. She strokes the fuzzy patch, almost disappointed by how smooth the hair lay.
	The doctors don't seem to notice, so she continues until they've pushed her down the hall, through a battery of swinging doors.
	Nurse Noris touches her arm and says, "No picking, okay?"
	Cindy nods and stops stroking the patch.
	When the nurse leaves, she carries on stroking but doesn't pick. Surrounded by dial-turning anesthesiologists and nurses, hand-washing doctors and pinging machines, she tugs--tempted--but refrains.
	The scene is already disorienting but when a soft plastic ring seems to grab her face she gasps, struggles. A funny smell.
	The doctor's old-man voice says, "Breathe, Cindy. Count backwards from one-hundred."
	Sinking fast, she tries to remember what number to start with.
	"Ninnnety--eigh--" and numbers fade to dancing sparkles. Down through the soft gauzy, she sinks.

Scene 5. CHOOSING BLADES

	His red beard had nearly reached the century mark, and had begun to grow gray. The smelly little chamber had been cramped as usual by a bolus of semi-dissolved cricket and mosquito carcasses. He had tried to dress the frog's stomach up a bit, even blocking his quarters off with a lovely swinging door made from dragonfly wings.
	But decades moving from toad to toad, frog to frog--and the odd toadstool now and then to be sure--well, that might have satisfied some gnomes. Not Stig. 
	He had practiced a great many crafts. Since his first apprenticeship cutting gold leaf for the Elder Council's shoehorns to his short flirtation with brandymaking, Stig had thrown himself into work. And though he excelled at these endeavors, boredom inevitably set in. 
	He was young, the elders told him. He would find satisfaction in the crafts in due time. If not brandymaking, then glass-cutting perhaps, or tinkery.
	Even his fourth-to-the-eighth granduncle, Magnus--whose kindness bordered on pederasty--had warned against overhigh aspirations: Patience, dear boy, and diligence. You can't carve Fanfantil's Comb in a century, you know.
	But none of the advice had touched that place of longing deep inside him. And only after long isolation in this toadsbelly had his own self-understanding crept in like slow fungus. It wasn't... mere accomplishment that he sought. The haughtiest craftsmanship was for naught if it served only the gnomish base need for finery. Toiling away at arts for its own sake was such a conceited practice. To what end? He might as well rot. And so he did.
	But the smoke attack had changed all of that.
	Not an hour after he'd pinioned his swinging doors in place, the toad's gullet was inundated by thick smoke.
	He'd yanked the doors shut, staring through their veiny clarity, wringing his hands, as the smoke rose like floodwaters.
	Before he could react, the whole chambe had shuddered around him and seized up, crumpling the doors and smashing Stig's face into the sticky bolus.
	He flailed as bile bubbled around his ankles, legs, hips. The bolus lurched up the gullet, dragging Stig along with it.  
	Through the esophageal canal, he was expelled, screaming. When he emerged, flailing onto his belly, he found himself perched upon a squishy pink expanse. The floor was soft and warm and ringed by shiny white monoliths protruding from ceiling and floor. The toad stared dull-eyed into Stig's besmirched face.

	Too late, Stig realized the toad's tongue had him by the bell of his shoe. Stig hugged the bolus for anchorage, but he was dragged into hideous daylight sun.
