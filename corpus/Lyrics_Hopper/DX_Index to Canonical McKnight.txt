Copyright Year: Not explicitly stated within the excerpt.

Main Characters:
▪
Narrator: Discovers the secret "Cottage language" of doilies.
▪

▪

Setting: "Hollers and dales" of "back woods stomping grounds" in "bluegrass country". A sunroom.
◦
Core Themes/Concepts:
▪
Hidden Knowledge Systems: The central concept of a secret "Cottage language" encoded in knitwork (specifically crochet), functioning as a sophisticated form of "record-keeping" and "representing thought" using "base-20 math".
▪
Familial Legacy and Secrets: The multi-generational transmission of esoteric knowledge within a secretive community.
▪
Linguistic and Information Theory: The narrator's efforts to "crack the code," realizing it's not a phonetic system but a different way of thinking. The idea of "information density" increasing at the "ruffly edges" of the knitwork, akin to marginalia.
▪
Cultural Identity: The distinctness of the "Cottage Culture" and its unique intellectual traditions.
▪
Perception of Randomness: The idea that "real random ain't as random-looking as one imagines" and that apparent randomness can hide deliberate patterns.
◦
Plot Highlights: The narrator discovers that his grandmother's seemingly ordinary doilies are actually encrypted documents of a hidden "Cottage language". He attempts to decipher this complex system with the help of Doctor Creights, learning about its unique mathematical and informational properties.
