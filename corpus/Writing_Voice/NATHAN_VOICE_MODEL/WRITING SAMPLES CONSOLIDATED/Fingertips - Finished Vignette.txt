TITLE: Fingertips

This was an exercize written for my writers' group. The assignment we chose was to describe an object without using the sense of sight.

-Nathan

(c) 2005 by Nathan McKnight

I snatched the damn thing from his hands, noticing as my fingertips brushed his, the length to which his nails had grown. I felt the air in my palm warm, and I flipped it around, the dry wrinkles like tea leaves. Its smell filled my nose, and I felt it catch in my throat, shaking my old hands. I dropped it and cursed. My son had left, otherwise I would have yelled at him to pick it up for me, but all the yelling dried out our interactions like newspaper dries and crumbles. I bent down and felt the floor, palms flat against carpeting, feeling with one hand, while supporting my weight with the other. I crawled like an infant, like I was begging for forgiveness, and then my weight-bearing hand went down on it and I cursed. The stench of melting nylon was strong but the pain in my hand was worse. Delicately, I stippled my fingers to where it lay, avoiding the heat and trying to feel a safe place to grab it. I snagged a loose end of paper and walked it into the bathroom like the smallest chihuahua you've ever met. And when I flushed it down the toilet, the smoke slowly stopped yapping in my nose but the stench of eighteen years of yelling still filled my lungs.
