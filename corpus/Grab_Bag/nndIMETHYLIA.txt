Image Generation Prompting FAQ
What are some examples of prefixes commonly used in image generation prompts?
Common prefixes include hyper-, ultra-, retro-, chrono-, non-, antediluvian-, psychotropic-, external-, wraparound-, recursive-, multi-, bicolor-, temporal-, supernova-, solar-, external-, cryogenic-, and synesthetic-.

How do I avoid multicolored terms like "kaleidoscopic" and "opalescent" in my prompts?
To avoid multicolored effects, focus on monochromatic, metallic, and solid-tone descriptors. Use words that emphasize defined, structured, and tonal materials or aesthetics for a grounded, coherent style.

Can I specify the aspect ratio of the generated image?
Yes, you can specify the desired aspect ratio using the --ar flag followed by the ratio, such as --ar 16:9 for a widescreen image or --ar 3:2 for a classic photo ratio.

How can I increase the level of detail in my generated image?
Adding descriptors like "8k," "dslr," "large-format," "gigapixel," or "photorealistic" can encourage the AI to generate images with a higher level of detail.

What are some ways to achieve a more realistic look in generated images?
Photographic styles and camera model names can guide the AI towards realism. Experiment with terms like "polaroid," "daguerreotype," "photographic," or specific camera models like "Rollei X2" or "Blackmagic Design."

What is the function of "::" in image generation prompts?
The double colon "::" is used to assign weights to specific parts of a prompt. For example, "Chair::300 background::1" emphasizes the "chair" element heavily while reducing the "background" element's influence.

What is the purpose of "--chaos" in image generation prompts?
The --chaos parameter controls the randomness of the image generation process. Higher chaos values (e.g., --chaos 100) introduce more unpredictability, while lower values (e.g., --chaos 0) produce more consistent results.
