OVERVIEW
The surprising connections between the clothes you wear and the computers you use.

Connecting anthropology, computer science, technology and engineering, Motif hits four out of five components of STE(A)M. With the potential of adding mathematics, it becomes a “Full STEAM” project. A perfect tie-in for the maker movement. 

There are some amazing opportunities for surprise and discovery in this course, so as much as possible, we should introduce topics without frontloading a great deal of background, and the program should be structured around ‘punctuations’ of surprise and ‘a-ha’ moments. 

OLD INTRO
A collaborative narrative using wearable electronics to explore the history of what we wear.

How traditional weaving technology made the computer possible.

The quest for automation led to programmable pattern-making looms. These programmable automated weaving machines got combined with automated calculating machines to make programmable automated calculating machines--aka computers.


CONNECTIONS
In 1801, Joseph Marie Jacquard made an improvement to the textile loom by introducing a series of punched paper cards as a template which allowed his loom to weave intricate patterns automatically. The resulting Jacquard loom was an important step in the development of computers because the use of punched cards to define woven patterns can be viewed as an early, albeit limited, form of programmability.

Design motifs are constrained by textile production methods

-weaving (patterns formed by varying color of perpendicular lines, often geometric)
	varies depending on whether it’s by hand, or using a loom (used for automated calculating, adding programmability = computer)
-knitting & crocheting (patterns formed by looping yarn or thread with special needles/hooks)
-braiding/plaiting (twisting threads together)
-knotting (tying threads together, as in macrame)
-lace
-knapping/piling (interlacing secondary yarn through woven fabric as in rugs, velvet, velour)
-felting (pressing threads together and working with heat and liquid to tangle)
-bark cloth (pounding bark until soft & flat)
