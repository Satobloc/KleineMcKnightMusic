The genetics broadly support the structure of what you understood. The major known forms of autosomal-dominant Alzheimer disease involve pathogenic variants in PSEN1, PSEN2, or APP. A carrier’s child generally has a 50% chance of inheriting the variant; if the parent truly did not inherit that familial variant, the child cannot inherit it through that line. Fifty percent is an independent chance for each child, not a requirement that exactly half of five siblings be affected. (NCBI)
And yes: age at onset can cluster remarkably tightly within a particular mutation and family. Research finds that the mutation and family together account for much of the variation in onset, and parental onset age is genuinely useful in predicting a carrier’s likely course. So an apparent family clock centered on forty-eight is medically plausible, even though “always exactly forty-eight” is a statement about your pedigree rather than a universal property of inherited Alzheimer disease. (Alzheimer's Journals)
The one technical asterisk is that your mother being healthy at fifty-five was not logically identical to a negative genetic test. Onset can vary, and rare carriers show unusually delayed disease. But in a family where the observed onset had been as tightly clustered as yours, every healthy year past forty-eight would have driven the probability sharply downward. By fifty-five, it was entirely reasonable for the truth to settle into you emotionally: the chain had probably stopped before reaching you. (PMC)

The Oreo Speedwagon Canon of Culinary Puns
Here’s a workable canon.
Oreo Speedwagon: Canonical Rules, Recommendations, and Leniency Measures
Core Rule
An entry combines:
a recognizable musician, band, composer, singer, musical act, or historically credited performer; and
a recognizable food, drink, ingredient, dish, brand, preparation, or culinary object.
The result must preserve enough of both sources to be recoverable.
The Primary Standard
The strongest entries make the fewest changes necessary.
Minimal edit distance is preferred, but not by itself sufficient. The result must also land quickly enough to be legible.
The ideal entry feels discovered rather than manufactured.
Examples:
Oreo Speedwagon
Art Garfunkugel
Gefilte Phish

Dual Legibility
A strong entry allows the reader to recover both components.
This does not require equal preservation.
One component may remain almost intact while the other survives only through a highly diagnostic trace.
Example:
Qui$he
“Quiche” remains almost fully legible. Ke$ha is represented chiefly by the dollar sign. The construction works because the food word is harder to alter without damage, while the artist can be recovered from one unusually distinctive marker.
The governing principle is therefore:
Preserve the more fragile component; reduce the other to the smallest cue that still retrieves it.
Spoken and Visual Legibility
Entries may work by sound, spelling, typography, spacing, punctuation, diacritics, or some combination.
Valid mechanisms include:
phonetic overlap
visual overlap

delayed realization

Canonical Recommendations

Avoid, when possible:

Leniency Classes
Class I: Canonical
Minimal change, immediate or strong delayed legibility, both components clearly recoverable.
Class II: Clean Stretch
One side requires a little interpretive assistance, but the mechanism is real and satisfying.
Class III: Baroque but Earned
Multiple steps, unusual vocabulary, or elaborate spelling, but every part contributes.
Class IV: Groaner
The construction is strained, but the strain itself is funny.
Class V: Frank Sinatra Hamburger
Arbitrary food attachment with little or no integration.
Class VI: Slop
The food does not exist, the musical source is misidentified, or the connection collapses under inspection.
Slop may be repaired, withdrawn, or ceremonially retained as a cautionary specimen.

The following are explicitly allowed:

The game rewards brilliance but does not require it.
Bad is legal.
Fake food is not automatically legal.
Adjudication Order
When evaluating an entry, ask:
Is there a real musical source?
Is there a real food or drink source?
Can both be recovered from the result?
What changed?
Was each change necessary?
Does the name preserve rhythm, silhouette, pronunciation, or some other strong identity marker?
Does the joke land immediately, later, or only after explanation?
Is the explanation revealing a genuine mechanism?
Is it an exact repeat?
If it fails structurally, is it at least a legally admitted Frank Sinatra Hamburger?
Final Principle
The highest form is not merely resemblance.
It is compression.
The best Oreo Speedwagons contain two complete recognitions in the smallest possible linguistic space.
This probably needs a short companion taxonomy of named failure modes and advanced techniques: Repizza, Frank Sinatra Hamburger, the Flan Flats, natural-born Oreos, delayed-legibility weapons, chain grafts, and terminal ontology.
