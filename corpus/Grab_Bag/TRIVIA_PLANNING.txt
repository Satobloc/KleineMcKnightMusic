PHILOSOPHY SPITBALLING	

In general, I want to capture the things that I find fun about trivia, discard some of the trappings that push me away from most bar trivia, and try to see how far we can push the format into new territories. 	
More fun, more creative, more than just showing off knowledge.	
Make the highbrow accessible	
Make the lowbrow intellectual -- *everything* is a potential resource in building an experience that feels like a thinkers' playground.	
Experimental and innovative	
Professional Weirdness	Organized, reliable, consistent delivery of weird, interesting, unexpected, and sometimes experimental EXPERIENCES in a slick and charismatic way. We should always be trying new things, keeping what works, and discarding what doesn't (or better, learning from the misses and trying a different way later). We should cultivate a sense that "anything could happen" when you come to our trivia night... but not to the point of being sloppy... fly by the seat of your pants, but spend some time building and installing some sturdy, fabulous wings on your flying pants before you jump off that cliff. Even if the wings fall off, try to make the crash spectacular to witness.
Curated Chaos	
Highbrow-Lowbrow and Lowbrow-Highbrow	
EVERYTHING IS INTERESTING	
Surprise their curiosity... turn the boring into the inspired.	Example: Announce a deliberately dull-sounding category title, like "Toothpaste" ...but make sure that the questions are so fun, so fascinating, and so impactful that that category is unforgettable... even poignant and inspiring if you can.
Curate expectations and reactions to orchestrate the experience start-to-finish.	Find ways to start engaging people the moment they walk in, or even before. Not just the usual ways of being welcoming and inclusive, but go a step further--or five or ten steps. Let's get creative and see how far beyond ordinary trivia we can get (esp since Pen & Paper sounds ordinary) Use as many tools at our disposal as possible to grab people's fascination by filling the experience with the unexpected, the personal, the fun. Example: Maybe turn all the table lamps upside down (with permission) and don't say anything about it until the category "Upside Down" is announced in the last round (better yet if it's possible to make this more than a callback... maybe we get clever and put a watermark on the paper that you have to hold over the light to see... or whatever. Weird for weird sake is good, but weird for the sake of intellectual curiosity is another level we can play on.)
