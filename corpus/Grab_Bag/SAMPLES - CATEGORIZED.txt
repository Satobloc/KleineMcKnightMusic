🕊️ LYRICAL-METAPHYSICAL
(Emotionally rich, symbol-laden, sensory or dreamlike introspection)

Petals on Electric Moon (Finished Vignette)
Summary: A poetic and haunting vignette that meditates on emotional distance, liminality, and the tension between memory and sensory presence. The style leans heavily into metaphor and soft disjunctions, like a dream remembered halfway.
Themes: Memory decay, artificiality, isolation, fleeting connection.
Notes: Feels kin to The Bird—likely generated or revised in tandem.

The Fifth Mattress (Unfinished Short, co-written)
Summary: A surreal tale in three braided segments: (1) a man stranded in Antarctica guided by a Brooklyn-accented penguin; (2) his childhood trauma at the hands of his violent brother Luke; (3) his imprisonment on a cruise-ship-turned-penal-colony. Each thread plays with memory, magical realism, and absurd horror.
Themes: Childhood trauma, escape, survival logic, unreliable identity.
Notes: Le Guin meets Kafka meets Adult Swim at -20°C.

🎭 SATIRICAL-REALIST
(Humorous, sharp, dialogue-driven, often grounded in institutions or relationships)

The Appetite (Unfinished Short)
Summary: A biting and grotesque satire of diet culture, social pressure, and internalized shame. Set in a DMV, with snappy, hilarious dialogue between Celeste and her coworker Belaune, the story mixes dark humor with creeping horror as Celeste’s body begins transforming post-surgery.
Themes: Body image, consumer pressure, female friendship, decay.
Notes: Starts like a sitcom, ends like Cronenberg.

Rocket Fuel (Finished Play)
Summary: A satirical barroom play for live performance featuring Buzz Awesome, a washed-up astronaut, and Peggy Chillax, a moonshine dealer. They're joined by the rotating archetype “Parade of Americans”—a hippy, politician, cop, etc.—who each comment absurdly on American identity.
Themes: National myth, intoxication, performative patriotism.
Notes: Structurally clever; Beckett meets sketch comedy with Americana edge.

The Hellfire Pigeon (Unfinished Short)
Summary: A teen coming-of-age tale in an alternate future where people send mail via roadrunners and breed animals as prestige. The protagonist finds an heirloom “hellfire jar” and uses it to impress a girl on a nighttime hangout. Under the playfulness lies real emotional vulnerability.
Themes: Legacy, teenage bravado, inheritance, flirtation and embarrassment.
Notes: YA-adjacent but emotionally grounded—Stranger Things by way of Vonnegut.

🌍 MYTHIC-FABULIST
(Allegorical, archetypal, drawing on myth or historical resonance)

The Gilgamesh Golem (Published Short)
Summary: An ancient clay golem recounts its life in post-9/11 New York. Interweaves Jewish mythology, urban decay, and political satire as Paul “The Maul” Stein confronts gentrification and Middle Eastern landlords. Epic tone with street-level stakes.
Themes: Creation, exile, empire, sacred vs. profane, cultural palimpsest.
Notes: Equal parts prophecy, protest, and prose-poem.

The Damps (Unfinished Short)
Summary: A prohibition-era tale set in coal country, where Mr. Eberts tries to offload beer before the mines—and the town—collapse entirely. Layered with ghost horses, underground fires, and economic desperation.
Themes: Industrial collapse, ancestral trauma, death of town culture.
Notes: Sherwood Anderson crossed with the Appalachian uncanny.

💣 POLITICAL-PARABOLIC
(Deals with societal critique, colonialism, or embedded systems of power)

Prick City Blues (Unfinished Novel)
Summary: Though fragmentary, the novel appears to follow multiple characters entangled in class warfare, generational burnout, and midwestern despair. The tone is gritty and absurd, with a satirical look at retail hell, crime, and economic precarity.
Themes: Anti-capitalism, sexual disaffection, paranoia, resignation.
Notes: Feels like Pynchon meets Bukowski, with a side of alt-lit.

👁️‍🗨️ EXPERIMENTAL-FRAGMENTARY
(Plays with form, erotics, or disorienting perspective)

Three, In the Morning (Unfinished Short)
Summary: A deeply intimate and erotically charged story of a woman caught between two men—her lover and his friend—in a sleepy, sensuous haze. The prose is intensely tactile, focusing on sensation and consent layered with ambiguity.
Themes: Consent, vulnerability, triangulated desire, altered perception.
Notes: Erotic but introspective—very voice-forward and mood-bound.


🕊️ LYRICAL-METAPHYSICAL
Fingertips (Finished Vignette)
Summary: A sensorially rich monologue from a blind narrator describing the world through touch and memory. Full of ghostlike longing and ephemeral perceptions.
Themes: Embodiment, loss, unseen worlds, language beyond vision.
Notes: Almost a prose poem. Quiet, aching, elliptical.

Crossingtide (Unfinished Short)
Summary: A contemplative meditation on identity and the oceanic self, most likely literalizing emotional transition and life passage.
Themes: Selfhood, migration, interiority, watery thresholds.
Notes: Vignette-like in tone; probably wants to become something longer.

Miss Poughkeepsie 1954 (Finished Flash)
Summary: A nostalgic but ambiguous glimpse into mid-century femininity and the performance of perfection, refracted through memory and possible regret.
Themes: Gender, image, time slippage, performance.
Notes: Quietly devastating. One of your most elegant flash pieces.

A Tealeaf from the Tangledays (Finished Flash)
Summary: A symbolically dense, fabulist micro-story about time, rituals, oracles, or ecological memory. A delicate miniature.
Themes: Cyclical time, natural knowledge, inherited intuition.
Notes: Reads like a story in a lost almanac or myth-cycle.

🎭 SATIRICAL-REALIST
The Appetite (Unfinished Short)
Summary: A DMV worker undergoes a grotesque transformation while dieting. Funny, disturbing, and painfully accurate about body norms.
Themes: Diet culture, grotesquerie, peer validation, invisibility.
Notes: The tone is comedic until it's not—perfect tension.

Indiana Jones and the Blog of Surprises (Published, 2006)
Summary: A sharp, winking post about masculinity, culture, and self-image, cast in the voice of a semi-parodic action-hero reflection.
Themes: Archetype subversion, nostalgia, performativity.
Notes: Reads like it wants to be adapted into a spoken word piece or video essay.

Chewie (Finished Short)
Summary: A strange, tender short about connection with an animal (or perhaps with loss itself), told in grounded but emotionally pointed prose.
Themes: Loyalty, death, inner pain refracted through care.
Notes: The ambiguity is a strength; feels real but symbolically deep.

Bent ‘N’ Dent, Parts 1 & 2 (Published, 2006)
Summary: A two-part flash satirizing late-stage capitalism and workplace absurdity, set in a Midwest discount appliance store.
Themes: Consumer fatigue, class performance, absurdity of value.
Notes: Strong stylization—feels like a sitcom by David Lynch.

Indiana Jones and the Collapse of the Patriarchy (Unfinished Blog)
Summary: A fragmentary piece mocking toxic masculinity and institutional decay, probably intended as a follow-up to Blog of Surprises.
Themes: Gender anxiety, power structures, ironic self-awareness.
Notes: Unfinished but sharply voiced; could be a great spoken rant.

The Gilgamesh Golem (Published, 2003)

Themes: Mythic inheritance, gentrification, exilic trauma, memory politics.
Notes: Feels like Wolfe meets Malamud by way of Borges.

Hatchlings (Unfinished Novel)
Summary: A novel-in-progress about children (or post-humans) emerging into identity through surreal and possibly alien conditions. Tonally dreamlike.
Themes: Becoming, metamorphosis, estrangement, outsider kinship.
Notes: Reads like Ursula K. Le Guin filtered through a child's nightmare.
