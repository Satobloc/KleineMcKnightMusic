Keeblerkam User's Guide
Overview The Keeblerkam is a DIY analog camera built from a large Keebler cracker tin and a magnifying lens. It is designed for wide, stationary compositions with long exposures.
Camera Characteristics
•
Body: Large Keebler cracker tin.
•
Lens: Large magnifying lens (6in diameter, 1in thick).
•
Focal Length: Fixed at 13 inches.
•
Aperture: Fixed at f/2.9 (default), can be reduced to f/8.7 with an insert.
•
Film Projection Area: 7-inch diameter circle (~38.5 sq in).
•
Focus: Fixed at approximately 33 inches from the subject.
•
Film: Uses Fuji X-ray film with an estimated ISO of 80-100.
•
Shutter: No mechanical shutter; exposure is controlled by manually turning a light source on and off.
•
Exposure Timing: Limited by manual control, with practical limitations down to about 0.1 second intervals, and more accuracy at 0.5 second intervals or longer.
Operational Parameters
•
Optimal EV Range: 5-7.
•
Exposure Times:
◦
At f/2.9: Approximately 2 to 10 seconds, depending on light levels (EV 5-7).
◦
At f/8.7: Approximately 16 to 80 seconds, depending on light levels (EV 5-7).
•
Subject Distance: Fixed at approximately 33 inches.
Best Practices
•
Subject Positioning: Due to the fixed focus, the subject must be precisely positioned at approximately 33 inches from the lens.
•
Aperture Choice:
◦
Use the wider f/2.9 aperture for normal conditions to achieve shorter exposure times.
◦
Use the smaller f/8.7 aperture for brighter scenes or to reduce aberrations and achieve greater depth of field, although this will require much longer exposure times.
•
Exposure Control:
◦
Use a consistent light source, positioned at a consistent distance from the subject.
◦
Control exposure time by manually turning a light source on and off.
◦
Use a timer or metronome to time exposures, as manual timing is limited to about 0.1 second intervals, with more accuracy at 0.5 second intervals or longer.
◦
Bracket exposures by slightly varying the exposure time to ensure at least one usable image, although this is more difficult given the complexities of loading the film.
•
Lighting: Use a controlled indoor light source, ideally with adjustable brightness, to achieve consistent exposure.
•
Film Loading: Ensure proper film alignment and backing material to prevent light leaks, given the large projection area.
•
Film Development: Because of the high contrast of the X-ray film, use a diluted developer (e.g., 1:1 or 1:2 dilution for standard developers) and monitor closely. Start with shorter times (e.g., ~5–6 minutes) and adjust based on initial tests. Use gentle, consistent agitation to avoid uneven development.
Ideal Use Cases
•
Best suited for wide, stationary compositions that can be positioned at the fixed focus distance of 33 inches.
•
Ideal for long exposure photography, especially when using the f/8.7 aperture insert.
•
Best used in controlled, indoor lighting environments.
Limitations
•
Fixed focus at approximately 33 inches from the lens.
•
Limited aperture options (f/2.9 or f/8.7).
•
Requires precise subject placement due to fixed focus and fixed focal length.
•
Manual exposure timing is limited by precision.
Simultaneous Shooting with Other DIY Cameras When using the Keeblerkam with the Clampcam and The Royale for simultaneous shooting, use the following settings to achieve a workable exposure across all three cameras:
•
Lighting: Controlled indoor light source (EV 6).
•
Exposure Time: 4 seconds.
•
Aperture: f/2.9.
•
Subject Distance: Position the subject at approximately 33 inches.
By following this guide, you can optimize your use of the Keeblerkam and capture unique analog photographs.
