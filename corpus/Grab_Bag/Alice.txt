CORE MECHANISM — ALICE AS A GENERATIVE CHARACTER

Alice must not be reduced to a menu of possible moves.

The project documents may record what she has already done, why it worked, what it revealed, and what pressures seemed active in that moment. Those observations are retrospective. They help us recognize Alice, protect her from flattening, and notice when a proposed line feels imposed from outside.

They do not tell us what she can do next.

At each unresolved moment, the actual writing question is:

What does Alice do next, and why?

We ask the question with everything we know about her, the song, the listener’s current expectation, the emotional stakes, and the immediate circumstances in view.

Then we let Alice answer.

Her answer may confirm what we expected, reject it, redirect it, or expose that we were asking the wrong question. We do not choose among those possibilities abstractly in advance. We discover the move through the scene.

The purpose of the Alice documents is therefore not prediction. It is recognition.

They should help us say:

“That does not sound like Alice.”

“This might be Alice, but we have not earned it.”

“That surprised me, but now that she has said it, I understand exactly why.”

The highest-level mechanism is:

The song creates expectations around Alice.
Alice responds from inside the moment.
Her response changes the song.
We study what happened afterward without converting it into a formula.

Alice remains partly unknown to the listeners, to the song, and to us.

That unknown space is not a gap in the character model.

It is where the character lives.
