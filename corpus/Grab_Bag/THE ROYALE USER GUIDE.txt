The Royale: User Guide
Overview The Royale is a unique, DIY analog film camera built from an antique wooden camera case and nested silver napkin rings. It is designed for experimental photography in indoor and low-light conditions. The camera lacks a mechanical shutter, and exposure is controlled by manually turning a light source on and off. It uses Fuji X-ray film, which has unique characteristics that require specific development techniques.
Camera Specifications
•
Body: Antique wooden camera case with nested silver napkin rings
•
Lens: Compound lens with a fixed lens behind an adjustable one.
•
Focal Length: Approximately 9.25 inches (235 mm) fixed
•
Aperture: Fixed at f/12 (approximately 0.75-inch diameter)
•
Film: Fuji X-ray film, assumed ISO of 100
•
Film Projection Area: 3.5 x 4.25 inches
•
Focus: Manually adjusted by moving the camera relative to the subject, with a range of approximately 1 inch to 35 feet, or potentially further
•
Shutter: None; exposure controlled manually by turning a light source on and off
Operational Parameters
•
ISO: Approximately 80-100
•
Optimal EV Range: 2-8 (dim to decently lit interiors)
•
Exposure Times (at f/12, ISO 100):
◦
Bright indoor lighting (EV 7): ~1 second
◦
Typical indoor lighting (EV 6): ~2 seconds
◦
Dim lighting (EV 4-5): 4-8 seconds
◦
Very dim lighting (EV 2-3): 8-16+ seconds
Focusing
•
The Royale's focusing mechanism is unique, relying on manually moving the camera relative to the subject.
•
The depth of field is shallow at f/12, so careful adjustments are needed to achieve sharp focus.
•
Adjust focus by moving the camera to bring the subject into the sharpest possible focus.
•
It may be useful to do test shots with the camera to determine best focus at varying distances.
•
The lens also has an adjustable outer lens element, but these methods are fairly coarse and imprecise.
Exposure Control
•
Exposure is controlled manually by turning a light source on and off.
•
The minimum exposure time is approximately 1/10th of a second, but more precise timing can be achieved at 1/2 second intervals or longer.
•
Use a timer or metronome to synchronize the light on/off cycles for more accurate exposures.
•
Exposure times are dependent on the lighting conditions of the subject, and the fixed aperture.
Film
•
The Royale uses Fuji X-ray film, with an estimated ISO of 80-100.
•
X-ray film is known for its high contrast, which can be mitigated during development.
•
The film is orthochromatic, meaning it is sensitive to blue and green light, but less responsive to red. This can affect how colors are rendered in black and white images.
•
The film may experience reciprocity failure, especially with long exposures.

Key Considerations for Use
•
Manual Control: The camera requires manual control over focusing and exposure.
•
Bracketing: Since bracketing is difficult given film loading constraints, aim for an accurate single exposure using the ideal parameters.
•
Experimentation: The Royale is designed for experimental photography, so testing and iteration are key to understanding the camera's unique characteristics.
•
Film Handling: The X-ray film must be cut to size and carefully loaded to avoid light leaks.
•
Subject Matter: Consider the orthochromatic nature of the film, which is less sensitive to red light. Red subjects may appear darker in the resulting images.
By understanding these parameters and practicing with the camera, you can successfully capture images with The Royale. Remember that each DIY camera is unique and requires a hands-on approach for optimal results.
