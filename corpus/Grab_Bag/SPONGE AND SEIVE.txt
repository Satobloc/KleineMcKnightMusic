A comment on a video he made that has a lot of similarities with my views, and I start at the beginning… And literally, this is my reply to the first sentence (which is about how online discussions get contentious but only because idea frameworks are using us to compete with each other):
We all have our own framework for understanding our reality. We all kind of know this already. But most people most of the time think that their ideas *are* reality. But that can’t be true. Because if it were then we’d all see the same thing exactly the same way all the time.
And yes, if we could look at how these ideas move and spread and interact… They would probably look a lot like an organism. The fact that idea is grow and evolve and compete is something that was first put into words by Richard Dawkins back in the 80s. He pointed out that ideas behave like genes, and he called them memes. And they get grouped together into meme complexes that behave a lot like organisms. “God” is a meme, a “religion” is a meme complex. A “song” is a meme, a “genre” is a meme complex.
Put different ideas into the world and behave in different ways. “The wheel” is a meme too. But it’s one we can physically build. The idea of a wheel can take different forms, but those forms are all very similar and function very much the same. A “vehicle” is a meme complex. And that idea has evolved from chariots to wagons to cars and trains and boats and airplanes and spaceships…
So you and I aren’t the first to look at things this way.
But although meme complexes behave a lot like organisms, they also behave very differently. The way organisms grow and reproduce and evolve it’s all pretty much the same. But meme complexes, all grow and evolve in really different ways from each other.
So it’s kind of hard to compare organisms with ideas. It’s not like apples to oranges… It’s more like comparing fruits to warfare. You can find ways to connect them, but it’s really a stretch and any metaphor you use is gonna fall apart really quickly. So fruits and warfare just aren’t the same kind of thing in any way that matters. Trying to talk about them coherently as if they were similar, becomes nonsensical very quickly.
So, comparing meme complexes to organisms… It’s a stronger metaphor, but it’s not exactly the same thing. Because idea, frameworks and organisms have *similar* behaviors, not the same behaviors. Organisms grow and evolve in different ways, but they all kind of follow the same rules. But the way ideas evolve… Every idea kind of follows different rules. Some of them are similar, but some very very different and it makes them hard to compare. The idea of a wheel has a built-in limitation to how far it can evolve. The rules of that meme allow wheels to be made of all sorts of different materials and have a lot of different properties… Some of them are hard, some of them are bouncy… But they’re all stuck being round. The idea itself forces that to be the boundary. A song follows rules, too, but not the same rules that the notion of a wheel follows. Because the idea of a song is defined by its internal structure, not its outward shape. Organisms are more like songs than they are like wheels, but they each following their own set of rules, so they are each very different kind of thing with their own constraints and limitations that make them what they are and determine how they can interact with the world. Songs and genres can have certain kinds of impacts. Warfare and apples have their own kinds of impacts. A song can hit you in a certain way. A car can hit you in a different way. So you can’t compare those impacts without recognizing how different they are.
The same as true we were talking about meme complexes, or idea frameworks. And trying to compare them to organisms. Kind of, yes.
But also kind of not. So that’s why I focus on the differences. The similarities give you a frame of reference and a starting point for understanding. The differences are what make the world dynamic. The similarities of the canvas. The differences are the paints.
Here’s my follow up to try to frame what I wrote:
So, I think we’re on the same page about the way our internal understandings are kind of playing a metagame competing with each other.
But I don’t think that’s why online discussions get contentious. I think they get contentious because we’re trying to protect our own framework because we think that’s what gives us meaning and so we resist anything that threatens to take our ideas apart.
I think maybe we’re all afraid that if we pick apart our framework, everything will fall apart. I just think it’s the opposite though. The difference between a car that drives and a car that doesn’t drive could be a hairline fracture in the head gasket. And the only way to make the car go is to pull up your sleeves and not be afraid tear it apart the engine and put it all back together again.
That’s the beautiful thing about ideas. No matter how much you tear them apart, they always put themselves back together again, and they’re usually more healthy afterwards.
And I think the last bit for now:
For me, the trick is to not feel attached to the framework. The framework is not me. The framework is something I use, and I’m always tearing it apart and putting it back together in new ways, throwing out the bits that don’t make sense, letting it reconnect… Picking up the pieces I threw out putting them back in somewhere else… That’s the fun of all of this to me. I never want to let the pain and fear of being wrong or being told I’m wrong to get in the way of the fun of recombining. Because you have to be wrong over and over again to ever find anything worth calling “right”. And I have to suppress the emotions that I feel when I’m too attached to my framework or else… It turns into contentiousness.
Actually… this is the capstone:
So, something that just occurred to me… and I think might actually be a good way to think about these things… You know, I think of myself is a bit of an idea of sponge, soaking ideas up constantly, squeezing them back out again and soaking up new ideas. However… that implies the ideas are disposable and get washed away. And that’s not true they keep going through the system.
So, I think a better metaphor for the idea, sponge is a real, living sponge in the ocean. I’m just talking this through as it’s occurring to me so bear with me but…
Something I learned in my biology or paleontology classes in college… Maybe you already know this, but it blew my mind:
Sponges take on all these complex and interesting shapes. It looks like a cohesive organism.
But you can take a living sponge, and literally force it through a sieve… And some of the individual parts will die. But then they’ll all come back together and form a sponge again.
So I think that we should always be ripping apart other people’s ideas and our own ideas, and recognize that we can just do that with a living framework. If it can’t survive that… It wasn’t alive in the first place.
And the coda lol:
That’s why your idea about frameworks being living things sort of shifts from being an idea about these organisms with their own goals, causing conflict… to a picture in which we, the organisms full of ideas, are the living sieves *and* the living sponges.
We just keep reworking each other, settling on one shape for a while, letting it evolve… And then letting it melt away to revolve into something different. Maybe better, maybe worse… We get to decide how long any particular configuration feels right. And we get to change it as much or as little as we like and no matter how much recombination we put the sponge through, we can never kill it and it can never kill us… Until we start taking it way too seriously
