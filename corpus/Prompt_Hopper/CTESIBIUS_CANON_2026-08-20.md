# CTESIBIUS CANON
## Canon freeze — 20 August 2026

This is the consolidated Ctesibius canon as of the end of the *Songs* / Chappell Roan hearing development pass.

**Compilation rule:** later corrections supersede earlier variants. Material that was merely floated, proposed, or explicitly left unresolved is separated at the end and is **not** promoted to canon.

---

# 1. Ctesibius

Ctesibius is an ancient Greek engineer/inventor who is also, in this mythos, a composer, rights-holder, catalogue manager, sometime co-organist, and extremely peculiar custodian of cultural provenance.

He is not omniscient about the future. He has access to future culture as if it were a gigantic, prematurely available, badly indexed catalogue. Release dates, languages, titles, metadata, translations, market divisions, and attribution can all arrive damaged or out of order.

His errors are therefore often **structurally adjacent to the truth**, rather than random.

Chronology is porous around him. Future works can be “on order,” prerelease dates can be centuries away, and a language can exist as a dependency that is “not even in beta testing yet.”

He treats all of this as routine.

---

# 2. Name and performance pronunciation

Greek name:

**Κτησίβιος**

For performance / Suno, use the deliberately speakable stage rendering:

**K'tay. SEE. BEE. Ohs.**

or, in a flowing line:

**K'tay-see-bee-ohs**

The initial K/T cluster matters conceptually, but the priority in generated audio is that the listener hears a stable, recognizable name.

A recurring irritation gag is that other people progressively butcher the name.

Example:

**LAWYER:** Mister Kattsaybeso—

**COUNSEL:** You’ve been corrected.

**LAWYER:** I’m doing my best.

**CTESIBIUS:** No.

# 3. Annoyed Ctesibius

When Ctesibius is genuinely annoyed, he stops spending language.

His answer becomes simply:

**No.**

It is not “No, you aren’t.”
It is not “No, that isn’t correct.”
It is not necessarily a grammatical answer to the immediately preceding proposition.

It becomes progressively **disemreferenced**. The room may no longer know which proposition, implication, person, premise, or portion of reality he is rejecting.

The ambiguity is the joke.

Example:

**LAWYER:** I’m doing my best.

**CTESIBIUS:** No.

Later, nested negatives allow multiple possible referents:

**LAWYER:** So you are not claiming that no one else has ever independently created anything resembling a Song?

**CTESIBIUS:** No.

**LAWYER:** No, you’re not claiming that?

**CTESIBIUS:** No.

**LAWYER:** Or no, nobody else has?

**CTESIBIUS:** No.

Eventually Counsel learns to exploit this:

**LAWYER:** What precisely are you denying?

**CTESIBIUS:** No.

**COUNSEL:** You heard my client.

# 4. Foundational organ scene

The foundational scene occurs around the first pipe organ / hydraulis on its first fully operational day.

A child wants to be the first person ever to hear it properly played.

Ctesibius is co-organist / engineer on the first fully operational day.

The child is intensely invested in **firstness**.

Ctesibius is not.

Canonical line:

**CTESIBIUS:** Nobody is first. We are just echoes passing through the holes.

The kid resents this answer because he did, in fact, want to be first.

This scene establishes the recurring tension between firstness, originality, prior art, transmission, licensing, cultural memory, and the difference between “the first person” and merely the first person whose paperwork survived.

# 5. Porous language and Beodonk

English is a future linguistic dependency from Ctesibius’s perspective and may be “not even in beta testing yet.”

He can nevertheless access or use it opportunistically.

**Beodonk** is the prerelease prequel to *Beowulf*.

Ctesibius has it on order, but the prerelease date is unfortunately still very far off.

This is not presented as time travel in a neatly mechanistic science-fiction sense. Future cultural material is simply accessible to Ctesibius through a badly synchronized catalogue / translation / metadata environment.

# 6. The protected category: *Song*

The formal visual title is:

**Songs^(τμ)**

or, typographically, *Songs* with lowercase Greek tau-mu in superscript.

However, **τμ is visual branding, not useful spoken lyric material**. For audio, the protected category is rendered simply as an emphatic proper-name-like:

***Song***

and plural:

***Songs***

The audience of a new song does not already know the mythos, so the Chappell Roan piece must explicitly establish the rule.

Canonical reveal:

**CTESIBIUS:** She is not a ***Song*** writer.

[beat]

**CTESIBIUS:** I own that word.

This does **not** mean Ctesibius owns all music.

Other people can compose, perform, write, chant, improvise, record, arrange, invent, or make whatever they like.

They simply cannot call the result a ***Song*** in Ctesibius’s protected sense.

There is therefore **no such thing as an independent Song** in his system. That is a contradiction. A Song belongs to the protected category.

Other people’s work can be called anything else.

Ctesibius does not care.

---

# 7. “Personal sonic accoutrement”

When pressed for what somebody else should call their independently made musical work, Ctesibius once offers:

**personal sonic accoutrement**

This is **not an official competing category**.

It is an offhand example generated because somebody insists there must be another term.

His actual position is:

Call it whatever you want.

Just do not call it a ***Song***.

The earlier phrase “personal acoustic enhancement” is superseded.

---

# 8. Authorship, licensing, and provenance

Ctesibius does **not** maintain that every later famous musician stole from him.

That would be crude and legally inaccurate.

Most famous later works in his orbit were **licensed**.

Historical records have simply lost, mangled, misfiled, mistranslated, buried, genericized, or otherwise corrupted the licensing trail.

This distinction is important:

- **Back catalogue:** historically famous works in Ctesibius’s catalogue, often legitimately licensed later.
- **Masterpieces / favorites:** works Ctesibius himself regards as top shelf.
- **Independent work:** genuinely separate work made outside the protected Song category.
- **Prior art:** work predating Ctesibius; absolutely respected.
- **Rights catastrophe / containment incident:** special cases such as *Tom’s Diner*.

Ctesibius does not need to insist that later artists are thieves.

Very often, when challenged, the later artists themselves confirm the contracts.

# 12. Frank Zappa

Ctesibius has historically, and erroneously, associated Frank Zappa with the children’s counting SING.

The current *Songs* hearing turns that old bad metadata into the first serious rupture in an otherwise astonishing run of corroborating witnesses.

Current canonical final-witness beat:

**CTESIBIUS:** I call my final witness. Frank Zappa.

**COUNSEL:** Mister Zappa, is it not true that my client, Mister K'tay-see-bee-ohs, generously provided the final line to your classic children’s SING, *one two three four five six seven eight nine ten eleven*... *gratis*?

**ZAPPA:** I did not write that song.

[beat]

**COUNSEL:** No further questions.

The key structural rule is:

**Everybody is on Ctesibius’s side until Zappa.**

The audience is trained to expect another absurd confirmation.

Zappa is the first factual problem.

Counsel simply drives over it.

# 14. Santa Claus

Ctesibius is neither believer nor disbeliever.

Canonical dialogue:

**KID:** Do you believe in Santa Claus?

**CTESIBIUS:** Strictly agnostic on the matter. Too many gods and minor deities running around these days. Why do you ask? Do I want to? How’s his cult?

The kid explains presents.

Ctesibius asks:

**And what sacrifices does he demand?**

The kid clarifies that Santa gives presents.

For free.

Ctesibius:

**What? For free?**

The scene remains unresolved.

Do not overdefine Santa’s ontology.

# 15. Tom’s Diner

*Tom’s Diner* is not artistic embarrassment.

It is a **containment / manufacturing incident**.

Too much **earworm powder** was used.

The hook became dangerously effective.

A recall was attempted.

The hook had already entered the population.

Some records were subsequently sealed or covered up.

Ctesibius becomes cagey about the event because it is a regulatory / liability matter, not because he thinks the work is bad.

Useful facts:
- formulation error
- too much earworm powder
- attempted recall
- failed containment
- population exposure
- sealed files / cover-up possibility

# 16. Nadeli Fire / Greek Fire

**Nadeli Fire** is the original product.

What history remembers as **Greek Fire** was licensed downstream.

“Greek Fire” later became the genericized name, erasing the original brand/provenance in much the same way a trademark can become the generic word for a product.

Ctesibius regards this primarily as a licensing / branding / metadata problem, not as “the Greeks stole it.”

Canonical attitude:

**Greek Fire? Nadeli. Licensed. Read the seal.**

or:

**Greek Fire was a trademark problem.**

The recent resurfacing / rediscovery of **H. nadeli** potentially complicates the inherited Hellenic provenance story.

The broader principle:

Cultural history is bad metadata layered over old agreements.

People translated badly.
Filed badly.
Remembered badly.
Marketed aggressively.
Confused distributor with inventor.
Lost the seals.

# 20. The pointer scenelet

This is the one portion of the song that should be allowed to breathe.

Ctesibius stands and presents his own case.

He has:
- a tripod,
- a large ordinary foamcore display board,
- and a strangely elaborate collapsible pointer.

The foamcore is important precisely because it is mundane office-supply material.

The pointer is overengineered.

The audio should let the extension / clicks / presentation register.

He shows the old title and the modern title.

He points to the location where the second T is absent.

Then:

**CTESIBIUS:** Prosecution rests.

**ADVISER:** We’re the plaintiff.

**CTESIBIUS:** Yes.

The scene should not be padded with explanatory dialogue once the joke has landed.

The pointer is later paid off at the end.

# 22. Defense terminology

The defense’s position is aggressively non-infringing.

Ms. Roan **independently** created a:

**SEECP**
= **Sonic Environment Enhancing Creative Product**

about “body positivity or whatever.”

Her creative process is described through:

**SING**
= **sonically-instantiated neural genius**

and the work can be discussed as:

**ACNPP**
= **Aural-Channel Neural Patterning Property**

These increasingly elaborate terms are **not Ctesibius’s demand**.

The lawyers invent them because they are afraid to use Ctesibius’s protected word.

Ctesibius would accept “music,” “composition,” “track,” “work,” or practically anything else.

He only cares about ***Song***.

# 25. Counsel’s conversion

Ctesibius begins the hearing representing himself, accompanied by a legal adviser.

The adviser initially expects mainly to prevent procedural catastrophe.

Then the defense makes the dangerous argument:

- there is no missing T,
- there never was a second T,
- but the non-presence of the second T materially changes the cadence,
- and that difference is part of what makes the later work successful.

The adviser realizes they have handed him a real legal trap.

He gradually becomes **Counsel** without the proceeding needing a formal ceremonial handoff.

The core turn:

**COUNSEL:** If it never existed, removing it should make no difference.

From then on he becomes increasingly delighted by the case while remaining lawyerly.

By the end, he is essentially a lead prosecutor in his own head, despite representing the civil plaintiff.

# 26. HOOK®

The key terminology escalation:

**Harmonic Obligation Oscillation Knurl®**

= **HOOK®**

The **K is specifically Knurl**.

A knurl is grip-enhancing texture, which makes it semantically adjacent to a hook / catch / grip.

The term arises when Counsel is constructing the phrase and Junior supplies:

**Knurl.**

The room pauses.

Then:

**COUNSEL:** ...actually.

Junior has gone from almost getting fired for saying “song” to potentially making partner.

Nobody explains who owns **HOOK®**.

Nobody asks.

**Null Knurl** was floated but is not canon.

# 27. What makes the T case dangerous

The defense insists:

There is no absent T.

There is no missing T.

Nothing was removed.

Ms. Roan simply never selected another T.

Yet the defense also wants the rhythmic / cadential benefit of the missing T.

That is the trap.

If something that never existed is compositionally operative, then the negative space itself has become part of the work.

Useful compressed logic:

**COUNSEL:** If the T never existed, removing it should make no difference.

**DEFENSE:** Nothing was removed.

**COUNSEL:** Yet it functions.

This is the point where Counsel “steps up to the plate.”

# 30. Everybody is on Ctesibius’s side — until Zappa

This is the crucial latest structural correction.

The joke is **not** that Ctesibius makes enormous claims while every real musician contradicts him.

The joke is that the real musicians keep **confirming him**.

One after another, witnesses and cameos validate contracts, catalogue arrangements, licenses, or independent-work distinctions.

The defense becomes increasingly desperate because the absurd provenance structure appears to be true.

Examples / current cameo logic:

- Lennon: licensed
- Dylan: licensed
- Bowie: back catalogue / licensed
- Axl Rose: still under contract
- AC/DC: genuinely independent and respected
- Enheduanna: prior art
- Sesame Street creator: genuinely independent and admired

Then Ctesibius calls his **final witness**:

Frank Zappa.

Zappa is the first one who says:

**I did not write that song.**

That is why the line lands.

# 37. *PRIOR ART*

*PRIOR ART* is a related / potentially separate Ctesibius song concept.

Its broad performance engine is battle-rap / procedural boasting.

Ctesibius does not need to claim that everybody stole.

His strongest flex is provenance.

Core conceptual posture:

- prior art is respected absolutely,
- later licensed work is licensed,
- independent work can remain independent,
- bad historical memory does not erase chain of title,
- a diss is merely corrective metadata.

Good banked phrases that fit his voice include:

- “This isn’t beef. It’s rights administration.”
- “Don’t call it a diss; call it corrective metadata.”
- “No feud. Just provenance.”
- “No beef. Just a discrepancy in attribution.”
- “You’re living in the past.” / “Where else would I keep the originals?”
- “You can’t own a word forever.” / “Correct.” / “So—” / “I own this one.”

These are **banked**, not all guaranteed lyric canon.

# 38. Character logic summary

Ctesibius is funniest when he is:
- literal about absurd legal categories,
- casual about impossible chronology,
- scrupulous about prior art,
- surprisingly fair about independent creators,
- uninterested in prestige hierarchy,
- pedantic about provenance,
- unreliable about damaged metadata,
- laconic when annoyed,
- and completely untroubled by contradictions that arise from everybody else’s terminology.

He does not need to posture as a villain.

He often behaves more ethically about attribution than the people challenging him.

The absurdity comes from the scale and age of his paperwork.
