# 11 — VOCALICS

Goal: reverse-engineer likely descriptive ecosystems that provide access to different vocal spaces; test strategies individually and in combination.

## Lexicon families

1. Musical performance: tessitura, onset, vibrato, portamento, melisma, phrasing, dynamics.
2. Ethnomusicology: social function, oral transmission, apprenticeship, ritual/labor/courtship, call-response.
3. Popular discourse: critic, fan, promo, producer, band-role and era language.
4. Phonetics/phonology: voicing, aspiration, glottalization, nasality, creak, stress, tone, reduction.
5. Phonotactics/prosody: legal sound sequences, syllable timing, cadence, pause and phrase-final contour.
6. Sociolinguistics: dialect, sociolect, register, class/age/occupation coding, idiolect.
7. Physiology: folds, closure, false-fold engagement, palate, tongue root, jaw, breath support.
8. Pathology/damage: diagnosis term vs anatomical consequence vs perceived acoustic effect.
9. Perception: papery, metallic, woolly, granular, age/gender ambiguity, social/emotional reading.
10. Nonmusical performance: poetry, stand-up, sermon, lecture, courtroom, auction, dispatch, acting.
11. Recording/media: wax, wire, cassette, scratch vocal, room/talkback, telephone, scanner, PA, codec.
12. Acoustic physics/space: formants, spectral tilt, reflections, occlusion, proximity, stairwell/quarry/car/blanket.
13. Natural/ethological: poetic analogies vs alarm/contact/territorial calls and survival function.

## Experiment method

Hold constant: lyric | melodic bed | model | settings | duration.
Vary one descriptive language at a time, then test selected pairs/triads.

Score separately:
TIMBRE | ARTICULATION | PROSODY | MELODY | BREATH | PERSONA | RECORDING | CONSISTENCY | DISTINCTNESS | CONTROL | SURPRISE
